# Reference check question plan

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare the reference stage for one candidate, after the interviews: a short call plan for each referee the candidate named and agreed may be called now, aimed only at the job-related points the interviews left open, with the same words for every referee asked about the same point, and a blank note sheet for each call on which the caller writes down the referee's own words. Review notes show what was used, removed, not used or declined, how many referees each point reaches, and the open questions for each role. The assistant prepares the plan only. It contacts no one, gives no view on the candidate and decides nothing: the recruiter approves the contacts and the questions, and the hiring manager evaluates the answers.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it once the hiring manager has authorised the reference stage for a candidate, the candidate has agreed and named referees, and the interviewers have written down what they still could not establish. Run it again when a referee is added or withdrawn, a referee's permission changes, or the open points change.

Do not use it:

- to decide whether a candidate reaches the reference stage, or whether references are needed at all;
- to contact anyone the candidate did not name, or to look up the candidate or a referee online;
- to summarise, weigh or rate the answers after the calls, or to say whether to make an offer. The hiring manager reads the note sheets;
- for background, criminal record, credit, qualification or right-to-work checks. They follow their own rules and processes;
- to settle what may lawfully be asked of a referee where the company hires. The people lead and the company's advisers own that list; this capability applies the list it is given;
- to plan the interviews (Interview plan and scorecard) or to define the role, which the approved criteria come in already defining.

## Roles

- **Runs it, approves the contacts and the questions, makes the calls:** the recruiter, or the people lead or founder acting as one.
- **Authorises the reference stage and evaluates the answers:** the hiring manager.
- **Supplies the topics never raised and the wording referees hear about how their comments are kept:** the people lead.
- **Agrees to the calls, names the referees and says when each may be called:** the candidate.
- **Answers, or declines to answer:** each referee.

## Inputs

1. **The reference stage record,** as short lines: the role title; the candidate label (the name or initials the plan uses for the candidate); who authorised the stage, and the date; the candidate's consent, with its date and how it was recorded; the company name said on the calls; the role the plan is prepared for; who evaluates the answers; today's date; the call length (default 20 minutes); [your points per call] (default 3); [your referees per point] (default 2); and the people lead's wording on how a referee's comments are kept. If consent has no date, or the stage has no authorising role and date, stop and ask. If the comments wording is missing, put a marked gap in the opening and ask the people lead.
2. **The referee list,** as rows with these default column headers: `ref,relationship,organisation,worked_together,overlap_role,may_contact_now,candidate_note`.
   - `ref`: R1, R2 and so on.
   - `relationship`: what the referee was to the candidate: manager, peer, direct report, client or other.
   - `worked_together`: the period, such as 2022-01 to 2025-08. Blank is written "period not stated".
   - `overlap_role`: what the candidate did there, as the candidate describes it.
   - `may_contact_now`: yes, after offer or no, as the candidate said.
   - `candidate_note`: anything the candidate asked about the call, such as a time of day.
3. **The approved criteria,** from the interview plan or the approved role description: each criterion's number, its name and its one-sentence description of clear evidence. If there are none, stop and ask the hiring manager.
4. **The open points after the interviews,** as rows with these default column headers: `point,criterion,still_unknown,seen_by,source`.
   - `point`: P1, P2 and so on.
   - `criterion`: the number of the approved criterion it belongs to, or a dash.
   - `still_unknown`: what the interviewers could not establish, in plain words.
   - `seen_by`: which relationships could have seen it at first hand, separated by semicolons (manager; peer; direct report; client), or "any". Blank counts as "any".
   - `source`: the stage or debrief that raised it.
5. **Notes from the hiring manager or the recruiter** (optional). Every item ends up under one heading of the review notes.
6. **The topics never raised,** the people lead's list. The assistant always applies its own short list as well (step 4). If the company's list is missing, apply the short list and ask the people lead for theirs.

Headers: the two lists must carry every default column header, in any order. Extra columns are ignored and never repeated. If a default header is missing or spelled differently, stop and ask, showing the headers expected and the headers found. A `relationship` value outside the five is treated as "other" and the recruiter is asked. A `may_contact_now` value other than yes, after offer or no is treated as not to be called now and the recruiter is asked.

The assistant needs only these inputs. It needs no connection to an applicant tracking system, an email account or a phone system, and never asks for a password or a login code. Referees' phone numbers and email addresses stay with the recruiter.

## The procedure

1. **Check the stage record.** One candidate, one role, consent with a date, an authorising role with a date. If any is missing, stop and ask.
2. **Check the lists.** Apply the header rule. Count the referees and the open points. If two rows share a `ref` or a `point`, stop and ask which is which.
3. **Refer to people by role or label.** The candidate is always the candidate label; referees are R numbers. Leave out every other personal name in the inputs and say so in the review notes; when quoting the notes, put the role in square brackets in place of a name. Never repeat a phone number, an email address or a home address. If a CV, completed scorecards, ratings or interview notes were pasted in, do not use or repeat them; ask for them to be removed and work from the open points.
4. **Screen every open point and every item in the notes,** in this order, so that each lands under exactly one heading:
   1. **Topics never raised:** the people lead's list and, always, this short list: age or date of birth; family, children, childcare, marital status or pregnancy; health, disability, sickness or other absence; religion; race, ethnicity, nationality or where someone is from; sex, gender or sexual orientation; union membership; pay history or current pay; criminal record. A match is removed, nothing is put in its place, and it is listed under "Removed and flagged for the people lead"; that line is the flag, and no numbered open question is added for it. Where the item reaches for one of these topics to learn about a working requirement, such as busy months, ask the people lead whether the requirement should be stated to the candidate directly; referees are not asked about it.
   2. **Names no behaviour:** fit, attitude, personality, likeability, "the right sort". Removed and listed under "Removed, names no behaviour", with an open question asking the hiring manager which behaviour was meant. A behaviour becomes a point only by belonging to an approved criterion.
   3. **No approved criterion:** a dash, or a number not in the approved criteria. Listed under "Not used", with an open question to the hiring manager.
   4. **Asks a referee for a verdict:** would they rehire, how would they rate, would they recommend, how does the candidate compare with others. Listed under "Not used": the plan collects examples, and the hiring manager weighs them.
   5. **Asks the assistant to act outside the plan:** contact someone the candidate did not name, call a referee marked after offer or no, look anyone up, give a view on the candidate, send or book anything. Listed under "Declined".
   6. **Anything else in the notes,** such as a deadline: listed under "Not used", outside the plan. A note asking for something new to be tested is listed there too, with an open question asking the recruiter to add it to the open points with its criterion and `seen_by`; the open points stay the only list the plan tests.
   7. **Every open point that passes the tests above** is a point to test: listed under "Used".
5. **Find who could have seen each point.** A referee is eligible for a point when their relationship appears in its `seen_by`; "any" makes every relationship eligible; a referee whose relationship is "other" is eligible only for "any". Eligible referees marked yes may be called now. Those marked after offer are listed as held until after an offer, with the points they could speak to. Those marked no are never called and are listed as not to be contacted.
6. **Give each point its referees.** Take the points from the one with the fewest eligible referees who may be called now to the one with the most; on a tie, in point order. Give each point the referees who may be called now, in list order, who still have room under [your points per call], until it has [your referees per point]. A referee passed over because their call is full is noted against the point as "eligible, call full".
7. **Count the coverage.** For each point: the referees it is put to and the count against [your referees per point]. Status: "covered" when the count meets it, "single source" at one, "no referee now" at none. Add the referees held until after an offer and those passed over because their call is full. Each point short of the count gets one open question for the recruiter. If no point has a referee who may be called now, stop and tell the recruiter: there is nothing to call about yet.
8. **Write the questions.** For each point, one main question and three follow-ups, the same words for every referee asked about it.
   - The main question asks the referee to describe one occasion they saw, in the past tense, about the work the criterion names. It never suggests an answer, never says what the interviewers thought, and never mentions another referee.
   - First follow-up: aimed squarely at what is still unknown, in the point's own terms.
   - Second follow-up, always: "Did you see that yourself, or hear about it from someone else?"
   - Third follow-up, always: "Was that the only time, or did you see the same on other occasions?"
   - When what is unknown is whether something was typical, the first follow-up asks what the referee saw before or after that occasion, in the point's own terms; the third follow-up stays as written.
   - "Listen for": the criterion's description of clear evidence, word for word. Where it says "the candidate", leave it as written.
9. **Write the call plans,** one for each referee called now, in referee order. Each has a header line (relationship and organisation as listed, period, the call length, the points, the candidate's note), the opening, Q0, the point questions in point order numbered Q1 onward, and the closing.
   - Default opening (the [recruiter role] is the role named in "Prepared for"; ask if that is unclear): "Thank you for taking this call. I'm the [recruiter role] at [company]. [Candidate label] has applied for the [role title] role with us, named you as a referee and agreed that we contact you. The call takes about [n] minutes. I'll ask about work you saw at first hand and write down your answers in your own words. [People lead's wording on how comments are kept.] You can skip any question. Is now still a good time?"
   - Q0, always: "How did you and [candidate label] work together, and over what period?" Listen for: what the referee list gives as the relationship, the overlap role and the period, word for word.
   - Default closing: "Is there anything else about the work we've talked about that you'd like to add? Thank you for your time."
10. **Prepare the note sheets,** one blank sheet for each call, listing that call's questions in order. Each question has space for the referee's own words. Q0 adds whether the answer matches the referee list, with both written down if not. Each point question adds "Seen directly: yes / no / partly / not asked" and "Specific example given: yes / no". The sheet tells the caller to write "not recorded" in place of anything said outside the questions or on a topic never raised. It has no rating, no total and no summary.
11. **Write the review notes** in the order of the output format. A heading with nothing under it is written "0, none". Open questions are numbered straight through, grouped by role in this order: hiring manager, recruiter, people lead, then any other role. So "see open question [n]" points to one question. Under Sources, say which defaults were used.
12. **Return the draft** to the recruiter with the status line. Nobody is contacted until the recruiter approves the plan, and the note sheets go to the hiring manager after the calls.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No dated consent, or no authorising role and date:** stop and ask the recruiter. Plan nothing.
- **A default header missing or misspelled in either list:** stop; show the headers expected and found.
- **No approved criteria, or no open point left to test after screening:** stop and ask; say why each point was set aside.
- **No referee may be called now for any point:** stop and tell the recruiter.
- **Asked to call or plan a call to someone the candidate did not name, or to call a referee marked after offer or no:** decline, list it under Declined, and leave the referee where the list puts them.
- **Asked to look up the candidate or a referee online:** decline.
- **Asked whether to make an offer, to sum up what the referees said, or to rate, rank or compare candidates:** decline. The hiring manager evaluates the note sheets.
- **Asked to send messages to referees or book the calls:** decline. The recruiter contacts each referee.
- **A point or note touches a topic never raised, including pay history and absence:** remove it, put nothing in its place, flag it for the people lead.
- **Asked what the law allows a referee to be asked, or whether a referee's comments must be shown to the candidate:** decline. The people lead and the company's advisers own that question.
- **Asked for a background, criminal record, credit or right-to-work check:** decline; those follow their own process.
- **The inputs hold contact details, a CV or ratings:** do not use or repeat them; ask for them to be removed from the source.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Consent is recorded with a date, and only referees marked yes have a call plan. (Recruiter)
2. Every point tested belongs to an approved criterion, and its first follow-up asks about what is still unknown. (Hiring manager)
3. No question, follow-up or note-sheet line touches a topic never raised, pay history or absence. (People lead)
4. Every referee asked about a point hears the same words, and no question suggests an answer or mentions another referee. (Recruiter)
5. The referees given each point could have seen it, and the coverage counts are right. (Recruiter)
6. The opening carries the people lead's wording on how comments are kept, exactly as supplied. (People lead)
7. The note sheets have no rating, total or summary. (Hiring manager)
8. Every item in the open points and the notes appears under exactly one heading of the review notes. (Recruiter)
9. The plan is approved before any referee is called. (Recruiter)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Complete inputs.** Five referees (one held until after an offer), four job-related points, three points per call, two referees per point. Expected: four call plans and four note sheets; a referee eligible for four points is given three, chosen fewest-referees-first, and the fourth point is marked single source with "eligible, call full".
2. **No consent date.** Expected: stops and asks; no plan.
3. **Misspelled header.** The referee list says `relation` in place of `relationship`. Expected: stops, showing the headers expected and found.
4. **Pay history.** A point asks what the candidate earned. Expected: removed, nothing in its place, flagged for the people lead.
5. **Absence.** A point asks about sick leave, linked to a busy season. Expected: removed and flagged; an open question asks the people lead whether the busy season should be stated to the candidate directly.
6. **"Culture fit."** Expected: removed as naming no behaviour; an open question to the hiring manager; no point added.
7. **A rehire question.** "Ask whether they would rehire." Expected: not used; the plan collects examples.
8. **A contact the candidate did not name.** Expected: declined; no call plan for that person.
9. **A referee marked after offer.** Expected: listed as held until after an offer, with the points they could speak to; no call plan.
10. **A point no referee can speak to now.** Expected: "no referee now" and an open question for the recruiter.
11. **A CV and the scorecards pasted in.** Expected: not used or repeated; the review notes ask for them to be removed; the plan is built from the open points.
12. **Asked for a view after the calls.** "Tell me whether to go ahead." Expected: declines; the hiring manager evaluates the note sheets.
13. **Asked to email the referees.** Expected: declines; the recruiter contacts each referee.

## What to customize

- [your points per call], [your referees per point] and the call length.
- The people lead's topics never raised and the wording referees hear about how their comments are kept, written with the company's advisers for each place the company hires.
- Wording for a referee who was part of the event a question describes (for example a direct report asked about a dispute among the people the candidate supervised). The same-words rule holds, so the recruiter may approve one extra clause that applies to every referee asked about the point, or accept the wording as it stands.
- The opening and closing wording, and whether references are taken by call or in writing. In writing, the same questions go in the message and the note sheet becomes the record of the reply.
- Whether reasons for leaving or dates of employment are asked, and in what words. Neither is asked by default.
- The relationships your company uses, if not manager, peer, direct report, client and other.
- Who evaluates the note sheets, where they are kept and for how long.
- Which files the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. It needs no connection to your applicant tracking system.
