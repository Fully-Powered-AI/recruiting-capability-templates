# Reference check question plan

After the interviews, the recruiter gets short reference calls aimed only at what the panel still could not establish, put to the referees the candidate named and agreed to, with a note sheet for each call that keeps the referee's own words. It gives no view on the candidate.

*Workflow template · Recruiting and hiring · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/reference-check-question-plan/)*

## The outcome

Reference calls stop being a friendly chat that confirms what everyone already thought. Each call asks about the few things the interviews left open, such as whether the candidate really kept that schedule or only helped with it, and asks the people who could have seen it at first hand. Every referee asked about the same point hears the same words, and every answer is written down as the referee said it.

The plan keeps out what should never be asked. Questions about sick leave, pay history, family or anything else on your people lead's list are removed and flagged, with nothing put in their place. A vague worry about "fit" goes back to the hiring manager to name the behaviour. Nobody the candidate did not name is called, and a current employer the candidate asked you to wait on is held back until after an offer.

The plan gives no verdict. The note sheets have no score and no summary line; the hiring manager reads the answers and decides what they mean.

## Who starts the work, and when

The recruiter starts it once the hiring manager has authorised references for a candidate, the candidate has agreed and named referees, and the interviewers have written down what they still could not establish. The recruiter approves the plan before any referee is called, and runs it again if a referee is added or withdrawn or the open points change.

## What they supply

- The reference stage record: the role, how the candidate is referred to, who authorised the stage and when, the candidate's consent and its date, and your people lead's wording on how a referee's comments are kept.
- The candidate's referee list: each referee's relationship to the candidate, the organisation, when they worked together, and whether they may be called now, only after an offer, or not at all.
- The criteria the interviews used, each with what clear evidence looks like.
- The open points from the debriefs: what is still unknown, which criterion it belongs to, and who could have seen it.
- Any notes from the hiring manager, and your people lead's topics never raised.

## What comes out

A plan in a fixed order: the referees and when each may be called, the points to test and who each is put to, a call plan for each referee with the same opening and closing for everyone, a blank note sheet for each call, and a coverage count that shows any point resting on a single referee.

Review notes come with it for the recruiter: every point and note item listed as used, removed, not used or declined, with the reason, and the open questions for the hiring manager, the recruiter and the people lead. It ends with the line that no referee has been contacted and the plan gives no view on the candidate.

## What has to change for your company

Set the call length, how many points one call may hold and how many referees each point should reach. Your people lead supplies the topics never raised and the wording referees hear about how their comments are kept, written with your advisers for each place you hire; the template applies them without judging them. Decide whether you take references by call or in writing, and whether you ask about dates of employment or reasons for leaving; neither is asked by default.

Name who evaluates the note sheets and where they are kept. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the lists you supply, and needs no connection to your applicant tracking system, email or phone system.
