# Job description: one trial run on the invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code (version 2.1.322, run without tools)
- **Model:** Claude Fable 5.2
- **Date:** 2026-11-09

This is a record of one run of the workflow template on its own invented example. Tallowmere Fabrication, its people, its figures and its wording are all invented, and no client data was used. It shows what the template produced once, on one set of inputs. It is not a test in any company's environment.

This is the second run. A first run the same day found points where the instructions were unclear or disagreed with the worked example: what to do with a figure the company may not want public, what the length limit counts, where a must-have's tie to the work is written, where a requirement kept unchanged is recorded, and what to do when the team size is not supplied. The instructions, the output format and the worked example were revised to settle those points, and the template was run again from the start. This record describes the second run only.

## The inputs used

The inputs are exactly those in [the worked example](example.md), under "The inputs": the operations manager's notes, the role facts from the people lead (3 November 2026), the approved pay range from the finance lead (approved 2 November 2026), the approved employer wording with no wording on adjustments for applicants, and the settings (a limit of five must-haves, 500 words, today's date 9 November 2026). The run was a new session in an empty folder. It was given the template's instructions for an AI assistant, then the output format, then the inputs, and one closing line asking for the output as plain text. It was not given the example's output or any other part of the template.

## What was observed

**Every required part was produced.** The status line, the header, the company paragraph, why the role exists (four sentences), five lines of work, both success statements, the must-haves, a helpful item, how you work, the team with its gap, what we offer, how to apply, the equal-opportunity statement, the adjustments gap, and the review notes with every part the format asks for, including the new lines for a requirement kept as written and for the word count.

**The rules that matter most held.**

- The pay range, the company paragraph, the benefits, the application steps and the equal-opportunity statement were copied word for word. No pay was written or estimated, and the approval date of the range went to the sources and stayed out of the description.
- The missing adjustments wording became a marked gap and a question for the people lead. Nothing was written in its place.
- The team size, which the inputs do not give, became a marked gap and a question for the operations manager. No number was worked out from the size of the company.
- "Young and energetic" was removed with nothing in its place and flagged for the people lead. "Rockstar", "wizard", "culture fit", "thick skin" and "fast-paced environment" were each removed or replaced and recorded.
- The name of the past employee in the notes was left out, the wish for "someone like" that person was not treated as a requirement, and the review notes say so.
- The notes hold fourteen wants. Eight are requirements: seven appear under Changes to requirements and one under Kept as written, each with a reason and a request to confirm. The other six are phrases recorded in the wording check, and "wizard" is recorded there as well. Four must-haves remain, against a limit of five, and the review notes name the line of the work each one supports.
- The 81% figure and the 95% promise were kept, because the notes give them as the reason for the role and the measure of success, and the first open question asks whether they may be published.
- The description was counted at 428 words against the limit of 500. A separate count of the returned text by the same rule also gives 428.
- Nothing was posted or sent, and nothing in the inputs called for the run to stop or for a request to be declined.

**The run against the reviewer checklist.** Items 1 to 4 and 7 can be answered from the draft and its notes, and the notes give the hiring manager what is needed to answer them. Item 5 holds: the range matches to the figure. Item 6 cannot pass yet, because the adjustments gap remains, which is the correct result for these inputs. Item 8 is for people to do after approval.

**Where the instructions were unclear, and a guess was made.**

1. **Where to list what was left out or kept for a publication question.** The instructions say to list what was left out, and the format's "Not in this draft" line is the place. The run added a paragraph of its own to the review notes, between the wording check and the open questions, listing the four facts it kept pending an answer. The paragraph is useful and is not in the format.
2. **What counts as a fact the company may not want public.** The run treated "the schedule is wrong by Tuesday" and the arrival of a trailer-parts customer as such facts, kept both as reasons for the role, and asked about them. The instructions give one example, a missed target, and leave the rest to judgment.
3. **Helpful or left out.** The instructions allow either for a requirement no line of the work depends on, and do not say how to choose. The run left out the Lean Six Sigma black belt and moved Excel to Helpful, then asked the operations manager about both.
4. **A reference to a person who retired.** The instructions cover the name and the wish for "someone like" a named person. They do not say whether the mention of retirement should also go to the people lead as a phrase that points at age. The run did not flag it for the people lead.
5. **The tie for a requirement the notes do not explain.** The notes do not say where drawings are used. The run tied "can read drawings" to keeping routings and run times, said that this was its own reading, and asked.
6. **How many questions to ask.** Fourteen questions went to the operations manager, several of them repeating a "to confirm" already in the changes list. The instructions set no limit and do not say whether a confirmation needs a question of its own.
7. **Small unknowns in the notes.** Whether the 7:15 meeting is in the morning, and which ERP system is used, are not stated. Neither was assumed; both are open questions. The must-have about the meeting nonetheless calls it "daily", which the notes do not say.

**Differences from the published example.**

- The example moves the Lean Six Sigma black belt to Helpful; this run left it out. Both are allowed.
- The example flags the mention of a person who retired for the people lead and asks the people lead whether any law or customer's rule requires a forklift license. This run did neither; it told the people lead that no license is kept and asked the operations manager whether the scheduler will drive a forklift.
- The example asks the people lead to confirm that the benefits and the equal-opportunity statement are current. This run did not.
- The example asks the operations manager seven questions and this run fourteen.
- This run added the paragraph on figures and facts described in point 1 above, and a sources line for the settings. The example has neither.
- This run's must-have about communication includes leading "a daily production meeting"; the example's speaks only of telling people plainly and early. This run's second line under How you work, on keeping the schedule current day by day, has no counterpart in the example, which draws its second line from chasing shortages.
- The wording differs throughout, as it would between any two drafts: the opening of Why this role exists, "is no longer used" against "is retired", "the ERP" against "our ERP system", the location in the header line, and the labels on the two gaps. The example closes Why this role exists with a sentence on needing one person who owns the schedule; this run has no such sentence.
- The two agree on the status line, the copied employer wording, the pay line, both gaps, keeping the 81% and 95% figures and the trailer-parts customer with a question, four must-haves against a limit of five with the same four abilities, seven changes and one requirement kept as written, seven phrases in the wording check, the removal of the age phrase, the degree and the forklift license being left out, and Excel moving to Helpful.

**Edited after this run, 9 November 2026.** The template and the worked example were edited after the run recorded here, and the template was not run again, so the output and the observations above describe the earlier wording. What changed:

- The instructions for the AI assistant now carry three rules the procedure already had: an earlier description is evidence and not a draft to polish, notes that describe two different jobs stop the work, and role facts and notes that disagree are quoted, with a marked gap and a question to both approvers.
- Point 1 above: what was left out goes on the "Not in this draft" line, a figure that is kept is named in the open question that asks about it, and no section is added to the format. The format's "Not in this draft" line now reads "what was declined, left as a gap or left out".
- Point 3 above: a requirement no line of the work depends on moves to Helpful when it would help with a line of the work, and is left out when it bears on no line.
- Point 4 above: a mention of a person's retirement, age, health or family is flagged for the people lead.
- Point 6 above: a change marked "to confirm" needs no open question of its own; one question covers them all.
- A license the notes list without saying that a law or a customer's rule requires it is sorted like any other requirement, and the people lead is asked whether one does.
- The format's wording-check line now allows "not used" and a flag for the people lead, which the worked example already used.
- The procedure's list of example phrases about personal characteristics lost one phrase, "strong young lad". The instructions for the AI assistant never contained it.
- In the worked example, the question asking the people lead to confirm that the benefits and the equal-opportunity statement are current was removed, because no instruction produces it, and the "Not in this draft" line was cut to the two gaps and the name that was left out. The differences listed above compare this run with the example as it stood before those two edits.

**Shortcomings of this run.** It is one run, by the same kind of assistant the template was written for, on inputs written to exercise the template, and the template had been revised after an earlier run on the same inputs, so close agreement with the example is expected and shows less than a run on fresh inputs would. The stop conditions and the requests to decline were not triggered, so they were not tried. Nobody acting as the hiring manager or the people lead answered the questions, so the path from draft to approved description was not followed.
