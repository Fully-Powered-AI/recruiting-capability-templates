# Job description

A hiring manager’s rough notes become a job description the right people want to answer: why the role exists, the work itself, and only the must-haves that are real. Your approved pay and employer wording go in untouched, and nothing is posted until the hiring manager and the people lead approve it.

*Capability template · Recruiting and hiring · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/job-description/)*

## The outcome

The people you most want to hear from read the first lines and recognize a real job: why it exists, what they would do all day, and what a good first year looks like. The list of must-haves is short enough to believe, because each one is tied to a line of the work. A manager with a team to run gets there in an afternoon, with last year’s recycled posting left in the drawer. That is how a growing company hires like one ten times its size.

It is a description of a role and nothing more. It is never an offer to anyone and never the terms of anyone’s employment. It assesses no one. Pay and employer wording stay with the people who own them, and the hiring manager and the people lead decide what goes out.

## Who starts the work, and when

The hiring manager starts it, as soon as a role is approved to fill or an old role reopens. The people lead, HR lead or recruiter reads the result beside the hiring manager. Both approve it, and then a person posts it. It also suits a role that has been open too long with few replies, where the old description goes in as one of the inputs.

## What they supply

- The hiring manager’s notes, however rough: why the role is needed now, what the person will do, what good looks like after 90 days and after a year, and what the manager believes is needed.
- The role facts: title, team, who it reports to, location, hours and working pattern.
- The approved pay range, from whoever owns pay. If it is not ready, the description marks the gap.
- Your approved employer wording: the paragraph about the company, the benefits list, the equal-opportunity statement, the wording on adjustments for applicants, and how to apply.
- Any earlier description of the role, if there is one.

## What comes out

One job description in a fixed order: why the role exists, the work, what success looks like, what you need, the team, what we offer and how to apply. It is addressed to the reader and stays inside the length you set.

Review notes come with it, for the two people who approve. They list every requirement that was moved or reworded and why, every phrase that was flagged, the open questions for each person, and where each section came from. Where an input was missing there is a marked gap, never a guess. The status line reads “Draft for approval. Not posted.”

## What has to change for your company

Name who owns pay and who owns the employer wording, and say where the approved versions live. Set your limit on must-haves, your section order, your length and your house style. Keep your own list of phrases to flag. Name who approves a description and who posts it, and where.

What the law asks of a posting differs from place to place, and that question stays with your people lead and your advisers. The template carries your approved wording exactly as you supply it. It does not judge it, add to it or stand in for it.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
