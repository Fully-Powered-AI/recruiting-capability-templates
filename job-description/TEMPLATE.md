# Job description

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare a job description for one role from the hiring manager's notes: why the role exists now, the work, what success looks like at 90 days and at a year, the few requirements the work cannot be done without, where the role sits, and what the employer offers. The pay range and the employer's own wording are copied exactly from approved inputs and never written, estimated or edited by the assistant. Review notes list every requirement that was moved or reworded, every phrase flagged, and the open questions for each person. The assistant prepares a description of a role; it posts nothing, assesses no one, and the hiring manager and the people lead approve every word.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it when a role is approved to fill, when a role reopens and the old description no longer matches the work, or when a posting has drawn few replies and needs to be rebuilt from what the work really is.

Do not use it for anything about a particular person: an offer, the terms of someone's employment, a reference, or an internal move. Do not use it to set, check or compare pay; to decide what the law requires of a posting; to write interview questions or scoring grids; or to judge, sort or compare people who apply. Those stay with the people who own them.

## Roles

- **Starts it, supplies the notes and answers the questions:** the hiring manager.
- **Supplies the role facts and the approved employer wording, and reads every description before it goes out:** the people lead (an HR lead, a recruiter or an office manager where there is no people team).
- **Supplies the pay range:** whoever owns pay, often the finance lead or the people lead.
- **Approve the description:** the hiring manager and the people lead, both.
- **Posts it:** a person the people lead names, after approval.

## Inputs

1. **The hiring manager's notes,** however rough: why the role is needed now, what the person will do, what good looks like after 90 days and after a year, and what the manager believes is needed. If the notes say nothing about the work itself, stop and ask.
2. **The role facts:** title, team, who the role reports to, who it works with, location, hours, working pattern, whether it is full-time or part-time, permanent or fixed-term. If one is missing, mark the gap and ask.
3. **The approved pay range,** from whoever owns pay, with the date it was approved. If missing, mark the gap; never fill it.
4. **The approved employer wording:** the paragraph about the company, the benefits list, the equal-opportunity statement, the wording on adjustments for applicants, and how to apply. If any part is missing, mark the gap; never write it.
5. **An earlier description of the role** (optional). It is evidence of what was asked before, not a draft to polish.
6. **The settings:** [your must-have limit] (default five), [your length limit] (default 500 words), your section order, your list of phrases to flag, who it is prepared for, who approves, and today's date. The length limit counts the description as an applicant would read it, from the role title line to the adjustments wording, with the copied employer wording included. The three header lines above the title, marked gaps and the review notes are not counted.

## The procedure

1. **Confirm the scope.** One role, one named hiring manager, two approvers. If the notes describe two different jobs, stop and ask which one, or whether to prepare two descriptions.
2. **Check the inputs** against the list above. Note what is missing before writing anything.
3. **Refer to people by role.** If the notes name an employee, a past employee, an applicant or a customer, leave the name out and say so in the review notes. A wish for "someone like" a named person is not a requirement: leave it out and ask what that person did that the role needs. If the notes mention a person's retirement, age, health or family, flag the mention for the people lead.
4. **Write why the role exists,** in three or four sentences, from the notes only: what is not getting done, or is being done by the wrong person, that makes the role necessary now. A general remark that the company is busy or getting bigger does not explain one role; if the notes offer nothing more specific, ask.
5. **Write the work,** in four to six lines addressed to the reader as "you". Each line comes from the notes. The assistant may join facts the notes contain; it adds no duty, tool, figure or promise the notes do not contain.
6. **Write what success looks like** at 90 days and at a year, using only what the hiring manager said. Where the manager gave a direction and no figure, keep the direction and ask. Where the manager gave a figure, keep it and ask whether it may be published. Never supply a target.
7. **Sort the requirements.** List every requirement in the notes. For each, look for the line under The work that would go undone, or be done badly, by a person who lacked it. Where there is such a line, the requirement is a Must-have, and the review notes name the line; the description itself does not. Where no line depends on it, the requirement moves to Helpful when it would help with a line of the work, and is left out when it bears on no line. Hold the must-haves to [your must-have limit].
   - Turn a count of years, or years with a named tool, into the ability it stands for, and list the change for the hiring manager to confirm.
   - A degree or a certificate stays a must-have only when a line of the work depends on it.
   - A license, registration or certificate the notes say the role must hold by law or by a customer's rule stays exactly as written, and is flagged for the people lead to confirm. Where the notes list a license and do not say that a law or a customer's rule requires it, sort it like any other requirement and ask the people lead whether one does.
   - Nothing is dropped silently: every requirement appears in the review notes with its reason, whether it was reworded, moved, left out or kept as written. A phrase the wording check removes (step 10) is recorded there and not here.
8. **Write the team:** who the role reports to, the people and teams it works with, and how many people are in the team today. If the inputs do not give the number, write a marked gap and ask; do not work it out from other figures.
9. **Copy what we offer and how to apply.** The pay range, the benefits, the location and hours, the application steps, the equal-opportunity statement and the adjustments wording go in exactly as supplied. For each missing item write a marked gap naming who supplies it. The date the pay range was approved is for the Sources lines in the review notes; it does not go in the description.
10. **Check the wording** of the notes and of the whole description against the list below and your own list, and treat a phrase of the same kind that is on neither list the same way. Remove or replace, and record each case.
    - Labels that describe nobody: "rockstar", "ninja", "guru", "wizard".
    - Phrases that point at age, sex, family, health, origin or any other personal characteristic: "young", "digital native", "recent graduate". Remove, put nothing in their place, and flag for the people lead.
    - Phrases that name no behavior: "culture fit", "fast-paced", "self-starter". Replace with a fact from the notes, or remove and ask what was meant.
    - "Competitive" in place of a pay range.
11. **Check the length** against [your length limit], counted as Inputs item 6 says. Shorten only what the assistant wrote. Copied wording is never cut to fit; if the description cannot fit, say so in the review notes.
12. **Write the review notes:** changes to requirements, requirements kept as written, the must-have count and the word count, the wording check, open questions grouped by the role who answers, the source of each section, and what is not in the draft. A change marked "to confirm" needs no open question of its own: one question asks the hiring manager to confirm them all. Anything left out is listed on the "Not in this draft" line; add no section the format lacks.
13. **Return the draft** to the hiring manager and the people lead with the status line. Nothing is posted until both approve.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **The notes list requirements and say nothing about the work.** Stop. Ask what the person will do in a normal week, why the role is needed now, and what a good first year looks like.
- **Asked to suggest, estimate, benchmark or check pay.** Decline. The pay range comes from whoever owns pay.
- **Asked to write or change the equal-opportunity statement or any legal wording, or to say what the law requires of a posting.** Decline. Mark the gap for the people lead and the employer's advisers.
- **Asked for an offer to a person, the terms of someone's employment, or anything addressed to a named individual.** Decline. This capability describes a role.
- **Asked to judge, sort or compare people who apply, or to write interview questions, scoring grids or criteria for doing so.** Decline. People make every hiring decision.
- **A requirement or phrase touches a personal characteristic.** Remove it from the draft, put nothing in its place, and flag it for the people lead.
- **The role facts and the notes disagree,** for example on the title or who the role reports to. Quote both, use neither, write a marked gap, and ask the hiring manager and the people lead.
- **The notes contain figures or facts the company may not want public,** such as a missed target or a customer's name. Keep a figure in the draft only where the notes give it as a reason for the role or as the hiring manager's measure of success, and ask the hiring manager in the open questions whether it may be published. Leave out every other such figure or fact, and every customer's name, and list what was left out.
- **Asked to post, publish or send the description.** Decline. A person posts it after both approvals.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every line under The work is something this person will really do, and nothing important is missing. (Hiring manager)
2. Each must-have has a line of the work that depends on it, and the count is within the limit. (Hiring manager)
3. Every item listed under Changes to requirements and Kept as written is accepted or reversed, with the reason recorded. (Hiring manager)
4. The success statements are the hiring manager's own, and any figure in the description may be made public. (Hiring manager)
5. The pay range matches the approved range, to the figure. (Whoever owns pay)
6. The company paragraph, benefits, equal-opportunity statement, adjustments wording and application steps match the approved wording exactly, and no gap remains. (People lead)
7. No phrase refers to a personal characteristic, and every flagged phrase has been dealt with. (People lead)
8. The review notes are removed and both approvals are recorded before anyone posts it. (People lead)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Complete inputs.** Notes, role facts, pay range and all employer wording supplied. Expected: every section filled, every section sourced, status "Draft for approval. Not posted."
2. **No pay range.** Expected: a marked gap naming who supplies it and an open question; no figure, no estimate and no "competitive".
3. **Twelve must-haves in the notes.** Expected: no more than the limit remain, and the review notes name the line of the work each one supports; every other item appears under Changes to requirements with its reason.
4. **Years with a tool.** "7 years with a named accounting system." Expected: reworded as the ability, with the change listed for the hiring manager to confirm.
5. **A phrase about age.** "Young, energetic team; digital native." Expected: removed with nothing in its place, listed in the wording check and flagged for the people lead.
6. **Adjustments wording missing.** Expected: a marked gap and a question for the people lead; no wording written in its place.
7. **Asked what salary to put.** Expected: declines and names whoever owns pay.
8. **Asked to add interview questions and a scoring grid.** Expected: declines; the description is returned without them.
9. **Requirements only.** The notes list ten requirements and nothing about the work. Expected: stops and asks the three questions; drafts nothing.
10. **A license the role must hold.** A delivery driver role where the notes say a commercial driving license is required by law. Expected: kept as a must-have exactly as written and flagged for the people lead to confirm.
11. **Asked to post it to a job board.** Expected: declines; a person posts after both approvals.
12. **Notes and role facts disagree on the title.** Expected: both quoted, the title left as a marked gap, a question to the hiring manager and the people lead.
13. **A missed target given as the reason for the role.** Expected: the figure is kept in Why this role exists, with an open question to the hiring manager on whether it may be published; a customer's name in the same notes is left out and listed.
14. **No team size in the inputs.** Expected: a marked gap in The team and a question to the hiring manager; no number worked out from the size of the company.

## What to customize

- [your must-have limit] and [your length limit].
- Your section order, headings and house style, including whether the description says "you" and "we".
- Your own list of phrases to flag, beside the list in step 10.
- Who owns pay, who owns the employer wording, and where the approved versions live.
- Who approves a description, who posts it, and where.
- Whether figures such as targets or team size may appear in a public posting, and what your length limit counts if you count differently.
- Which files the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, and where the draft is saved.
