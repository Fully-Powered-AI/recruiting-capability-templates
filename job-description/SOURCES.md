# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. Two openly licensed methods informed it, and the order and layout of the description follow them. From the job description method in panaversity/agentfactory-business-plugins (Apache-2.0): leading with the work, the header line of company, location and pay, the three-part layout of requirements (must-haves, helpful items and ways of working), holding the must-haves to the few the work depends on, never putting "competitive" in place of a pay range, always carrying the equal-opportunity statement and the adjustments wording, and checking the wording for labels and coded phrases. From the role description method in LeadMagic/gtm-skills (MIT): the section order from why the role exists through the work, what success looks like, the person, the team, what is offered and how to apply; opening on the specific reason the role exists now; and stating what success looks like at 90 days and at a year. The rules that pay and employer wording are copied and never written, that every change is listed for the hiring manager, and that nobody is assessed are Fully Powered AI's own. No third-party sentence is reused; a few short section headings and example phrases to flag are the same as in the sources.

- **Fully Powered AI:** The copy-never-write rule for pay and employer wording, the review notes, the handling of figures and gaps, the stop conditions, the tests and the worked example.

The method of writing to the reader and putting the work ahead of the requirements; the layout of the description's header line (title and team, then company, location and pay) and of its closing sections (what is offered, then how to apply followed by the equal-opportunity statement and the adjustments wording); the three-part requirements layout of must-haves, helpful items that are not required and one or two ways of working; testing each requirement for whether the work depends on it and holding the must-haves to about five; turning years of experience into the ability they stand for; never writing "competitive" in place of a pay range; always carrying the equal-opportunity statement and the adjustments wording; and checking the wording for labels and coded phrases, including the examples "rockstar", "ninja", "guru", "culture fit" and the age-coded "digital native" and "recent graduate".
