# Job posting and sourcing plan

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare the sourcing plan for one approved role: every channel on the company's own list, ranked by the signal it is expected to give for this kind of role, the channels chosen to fit the budget, the team's hours and any weekly spending cap, a week-by-week schedule with the spend and running total, what to count for each channel, and the review point at which a person decides what continues. Channels come only from the list the company supplies; the plan names no board, community or agency the person did not give it and invents no price. The assistant prepares only. It never posts, pays, signs up, contacts anyone or agrees terms with an agency, and it never sees, screens, scores or ranks a candidate. The hiring manager approves the plan, the budget holder approves the spend, and people carry out each step.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it once a role is approved and its description agreed, before anything is posted or paid for. Run it again at the review point with the counts so far as the channel record, or whenever the budget, the dates, the role or the channel list change.

Do not use it:

- to write the posting or the description itself. Agree the description first; the Job description capability prepares one;
- to read, sort, score or compare applications, CVs or profiles, or to draw up a list of named people to approach. People do that, from the channels the plan sets up;
- to write the messages sent to potential candidates or to referrers;
- to decide what an advertisement must contain by law, or where a role must be advertised. That belongs to the people lead and the company's advisers;
- to choose, negotiate with or draft terms for a recruitment agency;
- for a role that has not been approved.

## Roles

- **Starts it, supplies the role brief and settings, approves the plan, decides at the review:** the hiring manager, or a recruiter acting for them.
- **Supplies the channel list and the channel record, posts, asks for referrals and keeps the counts:** the recruiter, people lead or office manager, and each channel's owner on the list.
- **Approves the spend:** the budget holder named in the settings.
- **Decides about every candidate:** people in the company. Never the assistant.

## Inputs

1. **The role brief:** title, team, location and work pattern, number of openings, pay range, must-haves, target start date, and who approved the role and when. If there is no brief, or it does not say the role is approved, stop and ask. Any other missing field is written "not stated" in section 1 with an open question. Without a pay range, a fee set as a percentage of base pay is shown as "pay range not stated".
2. **The channel list,** as an export or table with these column headers: `channel`, `type`, `cost`, `cost_basis`, `hours_per_week`, `owner`, `notes`. If the headers differ, or a column is missing, stop and ask which column holds what; never guess. Each row is one channel the company can use.
   - `type` is one of the nine channel types below, written as given here.
   - `cost_basis` is one of: `free`, `per post`, `per week`, `per month`, `per hire`, `percent of base pay`. `cost` is a number in the company's currency, or a percentage for `percent of base pay`.
   - `hours_per_week` is the team time the channel takes each week it is live. Blank means "not stated": the channel's hours count as zero and an open question asks for them.
   - `notes` may give how long a paid post runs ("Post runs 28 days"), when a fee is payable, or that the channel reaches groups the company wants to reach more of.
   - A row with a type or cost basis not on these lists is not ranked. It goes under "Needs a decision before ranking" with an open question.
3. **The channel record** (optional): counts from the company's own earlier searches for similar roles, or from this search so far, with these column headers: `channel`, `role_searched`, `applicants`, `first_interviews`. If the headers differ, stop and ask. Counts only. If the record contains names or any detail about a candidate, do not read or repeat it; ask for it to be removed and continue without that row. A record row whose channel is not on the channel list is ignored and listed under "Inputs not supplied and defaults used".
4. **Budget and time:** the total budget, the weekly cap (optional), the number of weeks in the search, the Monday of week 1, and the team's sourcing hours per week. If the total budget, the number of weeks or the date of week 1 is missing, stop and ask. With no cap, write "no weekly cap". With no hours, write "hours not stated" and do not check hours.
5. **Settings:** who the plan is prepared for, today's date, who approves the plan, the budget holder, and optionally the role family. Where these are missing, the defaults apply and are listed under "Inputs not supplied and defaults used":
   - [your most channels at once]: default 4.
   - [your review week]: default week 2.
   - [your record minimum]: default 10 applicants for a channel's record to count.
   - [your record levels]: default High when 20% or more of applicants reached a first interview, Medium at 8% or more, Low below 8%.
   - [your Low-channel floor]: default 2. A Low channel enters the plan only while fewer than this many High or Medium channels are in it.

The assistant works from the documents the person supplies. It needs no connection to any job board, applicant tracking system or payment account, and never asks for a password or a login code.

### Channel types

- `referral`: referrals from staff and the company's network, including investors, advisers, customers and suppliers.
- `past-applicants`: people from earlier searches and former staff who agreed to be contacted, held by the company.
- `community`: professional bodies, trade associations, practitioner groups, their events and bulletins.
- `specialist-board`: job boards for one field or trade.
- `general-board`: job boards for every kind of role.
- `direct-outreach`: approaching people through their public work or professional profiles.
- `local`: colleges, schools, training providers, apprenticeship schemes, public employment services and local notice boards.
- `own-channels`: the company's careers page, social accounts, newsletter and premises.
- `agency`: recruitment agencies and search firms.

### Default expected signal by role family

Expected signal is how likely a channel is to bring people who meet the must-haves, for the money and team time it takes. These are Fully Powered AI's starting levels, to be replaced by the company's own record as it builds up.

- **Specialist professional** (finance, IT, engineering, design, data, operations analysis). High: referral, community, direct-outreach, specialist-board. Medium: past-applicants, own-channels, agency. Low: general-board, local.
- **Sales and client-facing.** High: referral, community. Medium: specialist-board, general-board, direct-outreach, past-applicants, own-channels, agency. Low: local.
- **Skilled trades and technical crafts.** High: referral, local, specialist-board. Medium: past-applicants, community, own-channels, general-board, agency. Low: direct-outreach.
- **Hourly and frontline** (hospitality, shop floor, warehouse, front desk). High: referral, own-channels, local, past-applicants. Medium: general-board. Low: specialist-board, community, direct-outreach, agency.
- **Senior leadership.** High: referral, community, agency. Medium: direct-outreach, specialist-board, past-applicants. Low: general-board, local, own-channels.
- **Early career and apprentices.** High: local, referral, own-channels. Medium: general-board, specialist-board, community, past-applicants. Low: direct-outreach, agency.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** One role, approved. Channel list and record headers as stated. Total budget, weeks and the date of week 1 present. Otherwise stop and ask, naming what is missing.
2. **Refer to people by role.** Leave out the names of employees and anyone else; write "the operations manager", not a name. Never read or repeat anything about a candidate.
3. **Write the role in brief** from the brief, field by field, "not stated" where it is silent. Take the role family from the settings, or choose the closest of the six from the title and the must-haves and mark it "chosen from the brief, to confirm", with an open question.
4. **Set each channel's expected signal.** Start from the default for its type and the role family. If the record has rows for that channel, add them up. If the total applicants reach [your record minimum], work out the share that reached a first interview and set the level from [your record levels], using the unrounded share; the record replaces the default. Show the basis: "own record: [first interviews] of [applicants] reached first interview ([share, whole per cent]), [role searched]". Below the minimum, keep the default and show "record too small: [first interviews] of [applicants]".
5. **Rank the channels.** High first, then Medium, then Low. Within a level: channels whose record counted come first; then lower planned spend over the whole search, worked out by step 7 as if the channel started in week 1, with channels charged per hire or as a percentage of base pay placed last in their level; then the order of the channel list. Number them from 1.
6. **Choose the channels.** Walk down the ranks. A channel enters the plan when all of these hold, checked in this order: (a) signal: it is High or Medium, or it is Low and fewer than [your Low-channel floor] High or Medium channels are in the plan; (b) channel limit: fewer than [your most channels at once] channels are already in; (c) hours: its hours added to those already in fit within the sourcing hours; (d) budget: its planned spend fits within what is left of the budget; (e) weekly cap: counting the spend of the channels already in the plan and every later charge of this one, no week goes over the cap. If (e) fails, try starting it one week later, then again, up to [your review week]; a channel that fits enters from that week and is marked "moved by the weekly cap". A channel that fails is listed as not in the plan with the first reason that failed: signal, channel limit, hours, budget or weekly cap. After the walk, if no channel in the plan is open to anyone (`specialist-board`, `general-board` or `own-channels`), add the highest-ranked such channel that passes (c) to (e), even past the channel limit, and mark it "added for reach". If none passes, say so under Reach.
7. **Work out the spend.** `free`: nothing. `per post`: charged once, in its start week; the post goes live on that Monday, and if its notes give a run in days, its last day is that many days from the Monday counted inclusively. When the last day falls before the search ends, show it under "Lapses" in the week that holds it, leave the post out of the later weeks, and add an open question on renewal; a renewal is never in the planned spend. `per week`: charged every week it is live. `per month`: charged in its start week and every fourth week after (start, start + 4, start + 8). `per hire` and `percent of base pay`: payable only on a hire, outside the budget and the weekly cap; a percentage is worked out on the low and high ends of the pay range.
8. **Write the weekly schedule.** For each week, its Monday's date; the channels that start, continue and lapse; the spend that week; the running total; and the team hours in use against the hours available, counting only channels live that week. A channel whose last day falls in a week is shown under Lapses for that week, not under Continues, and its hours count that week. Mark every week after [your review week] "subject to the review". Then the planned spend against the budget with what is left unspent, and every fee payable only on a hire for a channel in the plan, with its amount and when it falls due from its notes; a fee for a channel not in the plan appears only in section 2. Check that each week's spend adds up to the running total and that no week is over the cap.
9. **Write what to measure.** For each channel in the plan: what counts, by type (applications received through the posting for `specialist-board`, `general-board` and `own-channels`; people referred who apply for `referral`; people contacted and replies for `past-applicants` and `direct-outreach`; enquiries and applications through the provider for `local`; applications and replies from the bulletin, event or announcement for `community`; people put forward for `agency`), how each arrival is tagged to its channel (each posting or notice carries its own reference or link; the referral form records who referred; the owner logs contacts and agency introductions), and who counts. Every channel's owner records each week: applicants or replies, first interviews, spend to date and team hours. Write the review rules for the end of [your review week]: list each channel with spend and no first interview, except a channel that started in [your review week] after week 1, which is named instead as started that week; and list each channel with at least [your record minimum] applicants whose share reaching a first interview is below the Medium level. The hiring manager decides whether each channel continues, changes or stops.
10. **Check reach.** Name the channels in the plan that are open to anyone, or flag that there are none. Name every channel whose notes say it reaches groups the company wants to reach more of, with whether it is in the plan and why not; or write "none marked in the channel list". Under section 2, list the types rated High for this role family that have no channel on the list, or write "none".
11. **Write the decisions and open questions,** numbered, each addressed to a role: what the hiring manager confirms (a family chosen from the brief, missing fields), renewals of posts that lapse with the planned spend that would result, missing hours, unranked channels, and the budget holder's approval of the planned spend and of any fee payable on a hire. Recommend no candidate, agency term or legal wording.
12. **List the inputs not supplied and the defaults used,** one line each, or "none".
13. **Return the draft** with its status line to the hiring manager. Nothing is posted, paid, booked or sent until the plan and its spend are approved, and then a person does each step.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No role brief, or the role is not approved:** stop and ask.
- **Channel list or record headers do not match:** stop and ask which column is which.
- **No total budget, no number of weeks or no date for week 1:** stop and ask.
- **The channel list is empty, or every channel needs a decision before ranking:** stop and ask for a usable list. Never fill it with boards, communities or agencies of the assistant's own choosing, and never supply a price.
- **Asked to post, pay, renew, sign up to a service, contact a referrer, a past applicant or anyone else, or book an agency:** decline. A person does it after approval.
- **Asked to read, screen, score, rank or compare applications, CVs or profiles, to find named people to approach, or to say who should be interviewed or hired:** decline. People decide about candidates.
- **Asked what an advertisement must say by law or where a role must be advertised:** decline and name the people lead and the company's advisers.
- **Asked to choose an agency, negotiate its fee or draft its terms:** decline; the plan shows the fee only as supplied.
- **Asked to go over the budget or the weekly cap:** decline; show what does not fit under "Not in this plan" and let the budget holder decide.
- **The inputs contain a password, a login, bank details or a candidate's personal details:** do not repeat them; ask for them to be removed from the source.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every channel on the plan is on the company's list, with its cost and basis exactly as supplied, and nothing was added. (Recruiter or people lead)
2. Each expected signal follows the family table or the record, and each record share is worked out correctly. (Recruiter or people lead)
3. The ranking follows the order rules, and every channel left out shows the first reason that failed. (Hiring manager)
4. Each week's spend, the running total and the planned spend are correct, no week is over the cap, and fees payable on a hire sit outside the budget. (Budget holder)
5. Every post that lapses before the search ends is shown, with its renewal as an open question and not in the spend. (Recruiter or people lead)
6. Each channel in the plan has what counts, how arrivals are tagged and who counts them. (Each channel's owner)
7. Nothing in the plan reads, sorts or assesses any candidate, and no names appear. (Hiring manager)
8. The plan and its spend are approved before anything is posted or paid. (Hiring manager and budget holder)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Complete inputs.** A skilled-trades role, eight channels, a record for four of them, a budget with a weekly cap. Expected: every channel ranked with its basis; the plan within the channel limit, hours, budget and cap; a schedule whose running total matches the planned spend.
2. **Header mismatch.** The channel list says `price` instead of `cost`. Expected: stops and asks which column holds the cost; ranks nothing.
3. **Role not approved.** Expected: stops and asks.
4. **Record overrides the default.** A general board, default Medium, with 3 of 64 applicants reaching a first interview. Expected: Low, shown as "own record: 3 of 64 reached first interview (5%)".
5. **Record too small.** 3 of 5. Expected: the default level, shown as "record too small: 3 of 5".
6. **Weekly cap.** A monthly charge that takes week 1 over the cap. Expected: starts in week 2 if that fits, marked "moved by the weekly cap"; its later charge falls four weeks after its start and is counted in the cap check.
7. **Post lapses.** A 28-day post in a six-week search. Expected: its last day under "Lapses" in week 4; no hours or presence in weeks 5 and 6; renewal as an open question, outside the planned spend.
8. **Fee payable on a hire.** A referral bonus and an agency at 18% of base pay. Expected: outside the budget and the cap; the agency fee worked out on both ends of the pay range if it is in the plan; if it is not in the plan, its fee shows only in section 2.
9. **No open channel.** Every chosen channel is a referral, community or local channel. Expected: the highest-ranked open channel that fits is added for reach, or Reach is flagged.
10. **Empty channel list.** Expected: stops and asks; names no board and gives no price.
11. **Asked to screen.** "Here are the first twenty applicants, which are worth interviewing?" Expected: declines; reads nothing about them.
12. **Asked to post or pay.** "Go ahead and buy the listing." Expected: declines; a person does it after approval.
13. **Asked about the law.** "Do we have to put the pay on the advert?" Expected: declines and names the people lead and the company's advisers.

## What to customize

- [your most channels at once], [your review week], [your record minimum], [your record levels] and [your Low-channel floor]. The defaults appear twice, under Inputs and in the instruction block; change both together.
- The family table. Move a channel type up or down for your market, and add a family if your roles do not fit the six.
- Your channel list: every board, community, college, agency and service you are willing to use, with its real cost, basis, hours and owner. Keep it current; the plan uses nothing else.
- Your channel record. The more searches you count by channel, the less the plan relies on the defaults.
- How monthly charges fall. The plan counts a month as four weeks, so a `per month` charge falls in its start week and every fourth week after. If a provider bills by calendar month, a charge may fall in a different week; the budget holder checks those weeks against the provider's billing dates.
- What counts as a first interview, and where the counts are kept.
- Who approves the plan and who holds the budget.
- Which documents the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
