# Interview plan and scorecard: one trial on the invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-09

The inputs were the invented example in `public/example.md` (Fernhollow Bookkeeping, section "The inputs", parts 1 to 5: the approved job description, the hiring manager's notes, the interview settings, the topics never raised, and the settings), copied exactly. No client data was used.

This is the third run on the same invented example. Two earlier runs the same day showed where the instructions could not be followed as pasted or left room to guess; the template and the example were revised after each, and the run recorded here was made on the revised template. What the earlier runs found, and what was changed, is at the end.

The run was a fresh session started from Claude Code with no tools and no access to any file, in a folder outside the repository. Its instructions were the template's instructions for an AI assistant followed by its output format, exactly as the template tells a member to paste them, and nothing else from the template. The inputs were sent as one message. The session did not see the rest of the template, the example's own plan or the earlier runs. The same model, in another session, revised the template after the earlier runs.

## What was observed

**Every required part was produced.** All eight numbered sections and the review notes came out: two outcomes, five criteria, four stages, nine questions, a work exercise with a second attempt, coverage of two per criterion, four blank forms (seven per candidate), the rating scale and eight debrief rules. Nothing in the run made the assistant stop. Two requests were declined (the referral skipping a stage, the ranking out of 10), as the template says.

**The defaults worked from the pasted block alone.** The settings say "the template's defaults" for the timing and "the template's default" for the rating scale. With only the block and the output format to go on, the run applied the 15, 5 and 10 minute timing and the four rating levels with their meanings, including the meaning of "Not reached" on the exercise form, and said under Sources that each was the template's default.

**Handled as the template directs.** The five-year question was not used because it serves no criterion. The bank-statements question was rewritten about the past and listed for confirmation. The question about children and evenings, and the preference for someone younger, were removed with nothing in their place and flagged; the January long-days requirement behind the first became an open question for the people lead (open question 7). "Culture fit" was removed and the hiring manager asked which behaviour was meant, with no criterion added. The two employees named in the notes appear by role, in square brackets, where the notes are quoted. The adjustments question before Stage 1 is a marked gap because the settings refer to it without giving its words. Every opening says who is in the room, how long the stage lasts, that notes are taken and that every candidate is asked the same questions. The open questions are numbered 1 to 7 straight through, and every "see open question" points to the right one. Every item in the notes appears under exactly one heading.

**The panel note was treated as differing from the settings.** The notes name "one of the team"; the settings give Stage 4 to two bookkeepers. The run followed the settings, put the note under Not used with that reason and asked both approvers to confirm (open question 5), as the new rule for notes that differ from the settings directs.

**Where the template still left room to guess.**

1. How to spread criteria across stages. The run put all five criteria and five questions into the 60-minute Stage 2 and two questions into each 30-minute stage. The template gives no rule for how many questions fit a stage of a given length.
2. Which outcomes a criterion serves. The run linked following up (C4) to the year-one outcome only; linking it to the 90-day outcome as well is an equally fair reading.
3. The clear-evidence sentence for spreadsheets and software (C3) joins the two halves with "or", so either alone would count as clear evidence. The description says "comfort with spreadsheets and with learning new software". The template asks for the description's meaning but does not say what to do with a requirement that has two parts.
4. Where a name is replaced inside a quotation, the run kept the words around it, giving "[Senior Bookkeeper] (senior bookkeeper)" and "[Payroll colleague] in payroll". Readable, but clumsy.

**Departures from the output format.** The run added bold headings and a horizontal rule before the review notes, and wrote extra words into each closing ("We have time now for any questions you have for us.") and the exercise brief (the timing). None of these is in the format; none changes the content.

**Differences from the published example.**

- Stage allocation: both put C1 and C4 in Stage 1. The run puts all five criteria in Stage 2 and C3 and C5 in Stage 4; the example puts C1, C3, C2 and C5 in Stage 2 and C4, C3 and C5 in Stage 4. Both have nine questions and seven forms per candidate, and both cover each criterion twice.
- All question wording differs. Both rewrote the bank-statements question (the example as Q3, for running a process to a deadline; the run as Q7, for following up, and widened to anything an outside contact did not send), and both asked the went-wrong-or-late question in Stage 2.
- Review notes: the same items under the same headings (one used, one changed, three not used, two removed for topics, one removed for naming no behaviour, two declined). Both have seven open questions. The example asks the hiring manager what the five-year question was meant to show; the run does not. The run asks what candidates write the exercise email on; the example does not.
- C3's clear evidence: the example asks for a spreadsheet and software learned; the run accepts either (point 3 above). C4 serves O1 in the example and O2 in the run.
- Layout: the example prints the forms inside code blocks; the run prints them as plain text with bold headings.

**Facts in the output that are not in the inputs.**

- The reason given for widening Q7 ("because bookkeeping experience is helpful, not required") is the run's own reasoning, though it follows from the description.
- Open question 7 adds that the Client Services Manager "may also need to agree the wording", which is a suggestion, not something in the inputs.
- The exercise brief says the documents are needed "for their onboarding", a mild inference from the description, and asks for "a made-up client name", which is the run's choice.
- The clear-evidence sentences, the question wording and the openings are new text, as intended. None adds a standard the description does not state.

**Shortcomings of this trial.** One run, by one model, once. It shows that this model can follow the pasted block on this input. It does not show how other models or other roles would do, and it does not test the stop conditions that did not arise (no description, a CV in the inputs, a request about what the law allows).

## The earlier runs, and what was changed

**The first run,** on 9 November 2026 in Claude Code, used Claude Sonnet 5.5 (model ID claude-sonnet-5-5) and the template as first written. That run read the whole template, not only the instruction block, so it could see the default stages, timing and rating scale that the block referred to but did not contain. Its record noted:

- Whether the work exercise counts toward a criterion, and which, was left to judgement; it counted the chaser email toward writing and following up, where the example counted writing only.
- No rule for spreading criteria across stages or for how many questions fit a stage.
- No rule for notes that disagree with the interview settings (the panel note); it filed the note under "Not used", which was not quite what that heading meant.
- An item that is neither a request nor a question ("Want to start in two weeks"), and a "Not used from the notes" line under Sources that repeated the review-notes heading.
- Open questions grouped by role with no rule on numbering, so its cross-references were ambiguous.
- No meaning for "Not reached" on the exercise form.
- One note that mixed a topic never raised (children) with a working requirement (long days in January).
- The placeholder "[your questions per criterion]" left in the instruction block.
- A question that could serve two criteria, with the choice left to the writer.

It also compared its output with the example as then published. Its record states that the example had no open question asking the people lead whether the January long-days requirement should be stated to every candidate, and called this an omission. The example now carries that question as open question 6; it was added after the first run. Its record also found that the example's spreadsheet criterion (C3) and its question Q4 required a spreadsheet "that others relied on", a standard the job description does not state.

Whether the template's sentence "the work exercise counts once for each criterion it covers" was present during the first run cannot be established: no earlier version of the template was kept. The first run's point about the exercise concerns which criteria the exercise covers, which that sentence does not settle either way.

Changed after the first run:

- **The instruction block carries its own defaults.** The four default stages, the 15, 5 and 10 minute timing, the four rating levels with their meanings and the default of two questions per criterion are written into the block, which says to use each when its item is missing or set to the default and to say so under Sources. Only the remaining items (who decides, who approves, next-steps wording, standing wording, whether there is an exercise, scenario details) are marked gaps. The line before the block says the block carries these defaults. The placeholder in the block became "the number set for questions per criterion".
- **The review notes.** A Used heading; every item in the notes under exactly one heading; Not used defined to include a question that serves no criterion, a note that differs from the interview settings and something outside the plan such as a start date; the "Not used from the notes" line under Sources removed; open questions numbered straight through across all roles.
- **Notes against settings.** A rule: follow the settings, list the note under Not used with that reason, and ask both approvers.
- **"Not reached"** now also covers exercise work that did not get far enough to show a criterion.
- **Follow-ups** are asked in the past tense, and clear evidence adds no standard the description does not state.
- **The example.** C3, Q4 and its listen-for line no longer require a spreadsheet "that others relied on"; three follow-ups that asked what the candidate would do were put in the past tense; the start date moved from Sources to Not used; a Used line was added; the Sources line for section 8 now says the debrief rules come from the template.

**The second run,** on 9 November 2026, used Claude Opus 5.5 in a fresh session set up exactly as the run above, with the template as revised after the first run. It applied every default from the block alone and numbered its open questions straight through. It also showed three further gaps:

- Its openings did not say that every candidate is asked the same questions, because the block, unlike the procedure, did not list what an opening contains.
- Its review notes quoted the notes with the two employees' first names in them, and said that section must be removed before sharing for that reason; the block said to leave names out but not how to quote.
- It listed the mention of a referred friend as candidate information to be removed from the source, where the example and the third run list none. This was left as a judgement.

Changed after the second run, before the run recorded above: the block now lists the contents of each opening and closing, says to put the role in square brackets in place of a name when quoting the notes, says what to do when a criterion falls short of cover, and checks there is one role.
