# Interview plan and scorecard: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* Fernhollow Bookkeeping, its people, its role and every word of the inputs below are invented. It is an invented 40-person bookkeeping and payroll firm that serves small businesses and is hiring its first client onboarding specialist. The client services manager is the hiring manager; the office and people manager is the people lead.

The inputs come first, in full, so the template can be run on them. The interview plan and its review notes follow.

## The inputs

### 1. The approved job description

Approved by the client services manager and the office and people manager on 3 November 2026.

```
Client Onboarding Specialist, Client Services

Why this role exists: We take on 15 to 20 new clients a month, and
onboarding is done by whichever bookkeeper has time. New clients wait an
average of 19 days for their first monthly report. This role owns
onboarding from signed engagement to first report.

The work:
- You will run the kickoff call with each new client and agree the
  documents and access we need.
- You will chase missing documents and bank feeds until the file is
  complete.
- You will set up the client in our practice software and hand a
  complete file to the assigned bookkeeper.
- You will keep the onboarding tracker current and report weekly on
  where each client stands.
- You will improve the onboarding checklist as you learn where clients
  get stuck.

What success looks like:
In the first 90 days: you run onboarding for every new client without a
bookkeeper's help.
In the first year: new clients receive their first monthly report within
10 working days of signing.

Must have:
- Experience running a process with many people outside your team, such
  as clients or suppliers, to a deadline
- Clear, friendly writing in email
- Comfort with spreadsheets and with learning new software

Helpful, not required:
- Bookkeeping or payroll experience
- Experience with practice-management software

How you work:
- You follow up without being asked twice.
- You tell your manager early when something will slip.
```

- **C4 Follows up without being asked twice.** How you work. From: "You follow up without being asked twice."

- **C5 Tells their manager early when something will slip.** How you work. From: "You tell your manager early when something will slip."

Helpful, not required (noted if raised; no questions, no rating): bookkeeping or payroll experience; experience with practice-management software.

**3. Stages**

Before Stage 1: every candidate is asked, in the same words, whether they need any adjustments for the interviews. [Wording: to be supplied by the Office and People Manager]

- Stage 1: First conversation, 30 minutes, video, run by the Office and People Manager. Covers: C1, C4.
- Stage 2: Past work, 60 minutes, in person, run by the Client Services Manager and the Senior Bookkeeper. Covers: C1, C3, C2, C5.
- Stage 3: Work exercise, 45 minutes, in person, run by the Senior Bookkeeper with the Client Services Manager observing. Covers: C2.
- Stage 4: Meet the team, 30 minutes, in person, run by two bookkeepers. Covers: C4, C3, C5.

**4. Questions (the same wording, in the same order, for every candidate)**

*Stage 1: First conversation*

Opening: "Thank you for joining. This is a 30-minute video conversation with the Office and People Manager. I will take notes as we go. Every candidate for this role is asked the same questions in the same order."

- **Q1 (C1)** "Tell me about a piece of work you were responsible for that depended on getting things from people outside your own team by a deadline. What did you need, from whom, and how did it go?"
  Follow-ups: "What was your own part in it?" "Did it finish on time, and if not, what held it up?" "What did you take from it into later work?"
  Listen for: a specific process, the people outside the team, the deadline, and how it ended.
- **Q2 (C4)** "Tell me about a time you had to follow something up more than once to get it done. What did you do each time?"
  Follow-ups: "What made you follow up rather than wait?" "How did it end?" "What did you keep doing the same way afterward?"
  Listen for: follow-up the candidate started themselves, without a reminder.

Closing: time for the candidate's questions, then: "You will hear from us within five working days."

*Stage 2: Past work*

Opening: "Thank you for coming in. This is a 60-minute conversation with the Client Services Manager and the Senior Bookkeeper. We will both take notes. Every candidate for this role is asked the same questions in the same order."

- **Q3 (C1)** "Tell me about a time someone outside your team kept missing something you needed from them by a date. What did you do, and how did it end?"
  Follow-ups: "What did you try first?" "What did you do when that didn't work?" "Who did you keep informed while it was late?"
  Listen for: a specific process, the people outside the team, the deadline, and how it ended.
- **Q4 (C3)** "Tell me about a spreadsheet you built or kept up for your work. What was it for, and how did you keep it right?"
  Follow-ups: "What did you use it for from week to week?" "What went wrong with it once, and what did you do?" "What did you change in it afterward?"
  Listen for: a spreadsheet the candidate built or kept up for their work.
- **Q5 (C2)** "Tell me about an email you wrote that got a reluctant person to act. What did you write, and what happened?"
  Follow-ups: "How did you open it?" "What did they do after reading it?" "What did you learn about writing to people like them?"
  Listen for: an email that was clear and stayed friendly.
- **Q6 (C5)** "Tell me about something you were responsible for that went wrong or was going to be late. When did you see it coming, who did you tell, and what changed afterward?"
  Follow-ups: "How early did you raise it?" "How did the person you told respond?" "What do you do differently because of it?"
  Listen for: a delay seen coming and raised with the person who needed to know before it happened.

Closing: time for the candidate's questions, then: "You will hear from us within five working days."

*Stage 3: Work exercise*

See section 5.

*Stage 4: Meet the team*

Opening: "Thank you for coming in. This is a 30-minute conversation with two of our bookkeepers. We will both take notes. Every candidate for this role is asked the same questions in the same order."

- **Q7 (C4)** "Tell me about a time a task of yours was stuck waiting on someone else. How did you keep it moving?"
  Follow-ups: "What did you do first, and when?" "How did it end?" "What did you take from it?"
  Listen for: follow-up the candidate started themselves, without a reminder.
- **Q8 (C3)** "Tell me about the last piece of software you had to learn for work. How did you learn it, and how long was it until you used it on your own?"
  Follow-ups: "What got in the way?" "Who did you ask for help, if anyone?" "What do you do now when you meet new software?"
  Listen for: software the candidate learned and then used on their own.
- **Q9 (C5)** "Tell me about a time you had to tell a manager or a client that something would be late. When did you tell them, and how did they take it?"
  Follow-ups: "What did you say?" "What happened next?" "What did it teach you about when to tell people?"
  Listen for: a delay seen coming and raised with the person who needed to know before it happened.

Closing: time for the candidate's questions, then: "You will hear from us within five working days."

**5. Work exercise**

Stage 3. Covers: C2.

Brief, given in writing to every candidate: "A new client is three weeks late sending us the documents we need. Write the email you would send them today."

Candidates are given: the brief and [Scenario details: to be supplied by the Client Services Manager: which documents are missing and what the client was told at the kickoff call].

Said when the stage is booked: the exercise includes feedback and another go at the same task.

Timing: first attempt 15 minutes; feedback and questions 5 minutes; second attempt 10 minutes; 15 minutes for the candidate's questions and the closing.

Feedback: given the same way for every candidate. The Senior Bookkeeper points to two or three particular things in the first draft that could be better and takes any questions. The candidate then redoes the same email.

Each person in the room records: what the first draft did, the points given, and what is different in the second draft.

Opening: "Thank you for coming in. This is a 45-minute exercise with the Senior Bookkeeper, with the Client Services Manager observing. We will both take notes. Every candidate for this role does the same exercise in the same way." Closing: time for the candidate's questions, then: "You will hear from us within five working days."

Rating scale

- Clear evidence: a specific example, or work done in the exercise, that shows it, and what came of it.
- Some evidence: an example that shows part of it, or shows it in a smaller or simpler setting.
- No evidence seen: asked, and nothing specific came back.
- Not reached: not asked in the time, or, on the exercise form, the work did not get far enough to show it.

Forms in this plan

- Stage 1: C1, C4, one for the Office and People Manager.
- Stage 2: C1, C3, C2, C5, one for each of the Client Services Manager and the Senior Bookkeeper.
- Stage 3: C2, one for each of the Senior Bookkeeper and the Client Services Manager.
- Stage 4: C4, C3, C5, one for each of the two bookkeepers.
- 7 forms per candidate.

**8. How the debrief runs**

1. Forms are handed in before anyone talks about the candidate.
2. The interviewer with the least seniority gives their evidence first.
3. Start with the criteria on which the forms disagree.
4. Each view points to a specific answer or a piece of exercise work. A comment about fit that names no behaviour is put to one side.
5. Nobody counts hands. The Client Services Manager decides, after hearing the evidence.
6. A referral changes nothing: every candidate has the same stages, questions and forms.
