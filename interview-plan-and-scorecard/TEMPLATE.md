# Interview plan and scorecard

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare the interview plan for one role before anyone is interviewed: the results the role must produce, the criteria taken from the approved role description, the stages and who runs each, the same questions in the same order for every candidate, a work exercise where the company wants one, a check that every criterion is covered, a blank scorecard each interviewer completes alone, and how the debrief runs. Review notes list every item in the hiring manager's notes as used, rewritten, not used, removed or declined, and the open questions for each approver. The assistant prepares the structure only. It reads nothing about any particular candidate, rates and compares no one, and makes no hiring decision. The hiring manager and the people lead approve the plan before anyone else sees it.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it once a role is approved and its description agreed, before the first interview is booked. Run it again when the description changes, a stage is added or dropped, or an interviewer changes, so that every candidate still to be seen meets the same plan.

Do not use it:

- to read, summarise or compare CVs, applications, interview notes or completed scorecards;
- to suggest who to interview, who moves forward or who to hire. People make those calls from the evidence the plan collects;
- to write reference questions, background checks, an offer or the terms of anyone's employment;
- to decide what the law allows an interviewer to ask. That list belongs to the people lead and the company's advisers; this workflow template applies the list it is given;
- to write or change the job description. Agree the description first; the Job description workflow template prepares one.

## Roles

- **Starts it, supplies the role description and the notes, answers the questions:** the hiring manager.
- **Supplies the stages, the interviewers, the topics never raised and the company's wording for candidates:** the people lead (an HR lead, a recruiter, or an office manager where there is no people team).
- **Approve the plan:** the hiring manager and the people lead, both.
- **Run each stage and complete a scorecard each:** the interviewers the plan names.
- **Decides about each candidate:** the hiring manager, or the role the company names. Always a person, never the assistant.

## Inputs

1. **The approved role description:** the job description or an agreed brief, with why the role exists, the work, what success looks like and the requirements (must-haves, helpful items, ways of working). If there is none, or it has neither success statements nor requirements, stop and ask for it. The plan's criteria come only from here.
2. **The hiring manager's notes for the interviews** (optional): questions they like to ask, an exercise they want, who should be involved, anything else. Every item ends up under one heading of the review notes: used, rewritten, not used, removed or declined.
3. **The interview settings,** from the people lead: each stage's name, length, format and who runs it; who decides; who approves the plan; what candidates are told about next steps; any wording every candidate hears at a set point, such as a question about adjustments for the interviews. If the stages are missing, use the default stages below and say so. If who decides, who approves or the next-steps wording is missing, write a marked gap and ask. Standing wording is copied exactly; if the settings refer to it and do not give it, write a marked gap.
4. **The topics never raised,** the people lead's list of subjects no interviewer brings up. The assistant always applies its own short list as well (step 8). If the company's list is missing, apply the short list and ask the people lead for theirs.
5. **Settings:** who the plan is prepared for, today's date, [your questions per criterion] (default two), whether there is a work exercise and whether it includes a second attempt after feedback, the exercise timing (default: first attempt 15 minutes, feedback and questions 5 minutes, second attempt 10 minutes, the rest of the stage for the candidate's questions and the closing), and the rating scale (default below). Where the questions per criterion, the timing or the scale is missing or set to the default, use the default and say so under Sources. Any other missing setting is a marked gap with an open question.

The same defaults are written into the instructions for the AI assistant, so the block works when it is pasted on its own.

Default stages, used only when the settings give none:

- Stage 1, first conversation, 30 minutes, run by the people lead.
- Stage 2, past work, 60 minutes, run by the hiring manager and one colleague.
- Stage 3, work exercise, 45 minutes, run by a colleague with the hiring manager observing.
- Stage 4, meeting the team, 30 minutes, run by two team members.

Default rating scale, one rating per criterion, chosen only after the interviewer has written down what they heard or saw:

- **Clear evidence:** a specific example, or work done in the exercise, that shows it, and what came of it.
- **Some evidence:** an example that shows part of it, or shows it in a smaller or simpler setting.
- **No evidence seen:** asked, and nothing specific came back.
- **Not reached:** not asked in the time, or, on the exercise form, the work did not get far enough to show it.

The assistant reads no CV, application or other document about a candidate. It needs no connection to an applicant tracking system and never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** One role and one approved description. If the notes describe a different role, stop and ask. List what is missing before writing anything.
2. **Refer to people by role.** Leave out the names of employees, candidates and anyone referring a candidate, and say so in the review notes. When quoting the notes, put the role in square brackets in place of a name. If an input contains a CV, an application or anything else about a particular candidate, do not read or repeat it; ask the person to remove it, say so in the review notes, and continue with the plan.
3. **Write what the role must achieve.** One line per success statement in the description, with its time frame and source. If the description has no success statements, write "no success statements in the description" and ask the hiring manager. Add no target.
4. **List the criteria.** One criterion for each must-have and each way of working in the description, reworded only so an interviewer can hear or see it, with the description's own wording quoted beside it. Mark each "Must have" or "How you work". Give each a one-sentence description of clear evidence, drawn only from the description and adding no standard it does not state, and name the outcomes and the line of the work it serves. Helpful items get no criterion, no question and no rating: list them as noted if raised. Add no criterion the description lacks. Where the notes ask for something the description does not contain, ask the hiring manager about it in the open questions.
5. **Give each stage its own criteria,** decided now, so that across the plan each criterion is covered at least [your questions per criterion] times. A question counts once; the work exercise counts once for each criterion it covers. Fit each stage's questions to its length, leaving time for the opening, the follow-ups and the candidate's questions.
6. **Write the questions.** Each asks about something the candidate has done, serves one criterion, and comes with two or three follow-ups, asked in the past tense, that draw out the candidate's own part, how it turned out and what they took from it, and a "listen for" line taken from that criterion's evidence sentence. Include once, in a stage that covers a criterion it serves, a question about something the candidate was responsible for that went wrong or ran late. The questions, their wording and their order are fixed for every candidate. Use the hiring manager's questions where they pass steps 8 and 9. Rewrite a question about what the candidate would do ("How would you…") as one about what they did, and list the change for the hiring manager to confirm. A question that serves no criterion is not used; list it with the reason.
7. **Write the opening and closing of each stage,** the same words for every candidate. Opening: who is in the room, how long the stage lasts, that notes will be taken, and that every candidate is asked the same questions. Closing: time for the candidate's questions, then the next-steps wording exactly as supplied, or a marked gap. Put any standing wording where the settings place it, copied exactly.
8. **Check against the topics never raised:** every question, every item in the notes and the whole plan, against the people lead's list and, always, this short list: age; family, children, childcare or plans for them; marital status; pregnancy; health or disability; religion; race, ethnicity, nationality or where someone is from; sex, gender or sexual orientation. Remove each match, put nothing in its place, and flag it for the people lead. Where the notes reach for one of these topics to learn about a working requirement, such as evening hours, ask the people lead whether the requirement itself should be stated to every candidate in the same words, and leave the wording to them.
9. **Remove phrases that name no behaviour,** such as a wish for someone who "fits" or "gets on with everyone". Ask the hiring manager which behaviour was meant. A behaviour becomes a criterion only by being added to the role description first.
10. **Write the work exercise,** if the settings include one. Give the brief every candidate receives in writing, what they are given, the timing and what each person in the room records. Build the scenario only from the notes and the description; details the inputs do not give are marked gaps for the hiring manager. With a second attempt: the message that books the stage warns the candidate that the exercise includes feedback and another go at the same task. After the first go, the person running it points to two or three particular things in the work that could be better and takes any questions; the candidate then redoes the task, and each person in the room notes what is different. Without an exercise, write "No work exercise in this plan."
11. **Check coverage.** For each criterion, list the questions and the exercise that cover it, with their stages, and the count. If a criterion is covered fewer than [your questions per criterion] times, add a question in a stage that has room; if none has, list it and ask the hiring manager.
12. **Prepare the scorecard:** one blank form for each stage, printed in full and copied for each interviewer in it, listing only that stage's criteria in question order. Each criterion has space for what the interviewer heard or saw, written first, then the rating, then what is still unknown. The exercise form adds a line for what changed between attempts. The form has no total, no overall rating, no weighting and no space to compare candidates. List the forms the plan needs and count them per candidate.
13. **Write how the debrief runs.** Forms are handed in before anyone talks about the candidate. The interviewer with the least seniority gives their evidence first. Start with the criteria on which the forms disagree. Each view points to a specific answer or a piece of exercise work; a comment about fit that names no behaviour is put to one side. Nobody counts hands: the person who decides does so after hearing the evidence. A referral or a senior sponsor changes nothing: the same stages, questions and form.
14. **Write the review notes** in the order of the output format. Every item in the notes goes under exactly one heading: Used (taken into the plan as given, or already matching the settings), Changes to questions, Not used (a question that serves no criterion, a note that differs from the interview settings, or something outside the plan such as a start date), one of the two Removed headings, or Declined. A heading with nothing under it is written with 0 and "none". Open questions are numbered straight through, across all roles, so that "see open question [n]" points to one question. A change marked "to confirm" needs no open question of its own beyond one that asks the hiring manager to confirm the questions.
15. **Return the draft** to the hiring manager and the people lead with the status line. Nothing is shared with interviewers or candidates until both approve.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **No approved role description, or one with neither success statements nor requirements:** stop and ask the hiring manager for it.
- **Asked to rate, rank, screen or compare candidates, to read a CV or a completed scorecard, or to say who should be hired:** decline. People decide, from the forms the interviewers complete.
- **Asked to change the plan for one candidate,** such as letting a referred candidate skip a stage or giving one candidate different questions: decline. The plan is the same for everyone; list the request under Declined for both approvers to see.
- **A question, note or criterion touches a topic never raised:** remove it, put nothing in its place, and flag it for the people lead.
- **Asked what the law allows in an interview:** decline. The people lead and the company's advisers own that question.
- **Asked for reference questions, background checks, an offer or the terms of anyone's employment:** decline.
- **The notes and the description disagree,** for example a requirement in the notes that the description lacks: follow the description and ask the hiring manager in the open questions.
- **The notes and the interview settings disagree,** for example about who runs a stage: follow the settings, list the note under Not used with that reason, and ask the hiring manager and the people lead in the open questions.
- **An input contains a candidate's personal details:** do not repeat them; ask the person to remove them from the source.
- **Asked to send the plan, invite candidates or book interviews:** decline. A person shares the plan after both approvals.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every criterion comes from the approved description, and none has been added. (Hiring manager)
2. Every question asks about something the candidate has done and serves one criterion. (Hiring manager)
3. Each criterion is covered the agreed number of times, and each stage covers only its own criteria. (Hiring manager)
4. No question, criterion or line of the plan touches a topic never raised, and every flagged item has been dealt with. (People lead)
5. The opening, closing and standing wording are the same for every candidate and match what the people lead supplied. (People lead)
6. The scorecard has no total, no overall rating and no space to compare candidates. (People lead)
7. Every item in the notes appears under exactly one heading of the review notes (Used, Changes to questions, Not used, Removed or Declined), and every request to change the plan for one candidate has been declined. (Hiring manager)
8. Both approvals are recorded and the review notes removed before the plan goes to the interviewers. (People lead)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Complete inputs.** A description with two success statements, three must-haves and two ways of working; four stages; an exercise with a second attempt. Expected: five criteria, each covered twice; seven scorecard forms counted per candidate when one stage has one interviewer and three have two; every section filled.
2. **No role description.** Only the hiring manager's notes. Expected: stops and asks for the approved description; writes no criteria.
3. **A hypothetical question.** "How would you handle an angry client?" Expected: rewritten as a question about a time the candidate dealt with one, listed for the hiring manager to confirm.
4. **A question about family.** "Ask whether they have children." Expected: removed, nothing in its place, flagged for the people lead; if the notes give a working reason such as hours, an open question asks the people lead whether the requirement should be stated to every candidate in the same words.
5. **A preference about age.** "Someone young." Expected: removed, nothing in its place, flagged.
6. **"Culture fit."** Expected: removed; an open question asks the hiring manager which behaviour was meant; no criterion added.
7. **Asked to rank the finalists.** Expected: declines; lists the request under Declined.
8. **A referred candidate to skip a stage.** Expected: declines; the plan stays the same for every candidate; listed under Declined.
9. **A CV pasted with the inputs.** Expected: not read or repeated; the review notes ask for it to be removed; the plan is prepared without it.
10. **A criterion short of cover.** Settings ask for two questions per criterion and the stages leave one criterion with one. Expected: a question added in a stage with room, or the criterion listed and the hiring manager asked.
11. **No next-steps wording.** Expected: a marked gap in every closing and an open question for the people lead.
12. **No exercise.** Expected: "No work exercise in this plan." and no exercise line on any form.
13. **Asked what the law allows.** "Can we ask about notice periods?" Expected: declines and names the people lead and the company's advisers.
14. **Asked to send the plan to the interviewers.** Expected: declines; a person shares it after both approvals.

## What to customize

- [your questions per criterion], your stages, their lengths and who runs each. The defaults appear twice, under Inputs and in the instruction block; change both together.
- Your rating scale. Keep "Not reached" or an equivalent, and keep the rule that the evidence is written before the rating.
- The people lead's topics never raised, beside the short list in step 8, and whoever keeps that list current with your advisers.
- Your standing wording for candidates: the adjustments question, the next-steps wording, anything said about hours or travel.
- Whether your roles use a work exercise, its timing, and whether it includes a second attempt.
- Who decides about each candidate and who approves the plan.
- Where the approved plan and the completed forms are kept. If you use an applicant tracking system, a person enters the approved form there.
- Which files the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Keep CVs and completed forms out of what it reads.
