# Work sample exercise brief: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* Studio B, its people, its role, its clients and every word of the inputs below are invented. Studio B is an invented 30-person marketing studio hiring its first Project Coordinator. The Head of Delivery is the hiring manager; the Office Manager is the people lead.

The inputs come first, in full, so the template can be run on them. The brief follows.

## The inputs

### 1. The approved role description

Approved by the Head of Delivery and the Office Manager on 2 November 2026.

```
Project Coordinator, Delivery

Why this role exists: Studio B runs about 25 client projects at a time,
and each project manager keeps their own schedule. Changes from clients
reach the schedule late. This role keeps every schedule current and
tells clients plainly what a change means.

The work:
- You will build and update project schedules from kickoff notes.
- You will turn client change requests into revised timelines.
- You will write the weekly status email to each client.
- You will chase overdue client feedback.
- You will hand finished projects to invoicing.

What success looks like:
In the first 90 days: every schedule is current each Monday.
In the first year: no client hears about a delay from anyone but us.
```

### 2. The approved criteria

Approved by the Head of Delivery on 5 November 2026.

```
C1 Plans work in a realistic order.
   Clear evidence: a timeline in which each step starts after the step
   it depends on has finished, with dates.
C2 Notices what is missing or contradictory and says so.
   Clear evidence: names a gap or a clash in the information given and
   asks about it or states an assumption.
C3 Writes clear, friendly messages to clients.
   Clear evidence: a message that says what changes and what is needed
   from the client, and stays polite.
C4 Keeps dates and numbers accurate.
   Clear evidence: dates fall on working days, and the figures agree
   between the parts of the work.
C5 Keeps several client relationships moving at once.
   Clear evidence: a specific time the person juggled several clients'
   needs and what happened.
```

## The output

**WORK SAMPLE EXERCISE BRIEF: Project Coordinator**

Status: Draft for approval. Not shared with candidates or the panel.
Prepared for: Head of Delivery. Approves: Head of Delivery. Panel: Head of Delivery; Senior Project Manager. People lead: Office Manager. Decides about each candidate: Head of Delivery. Date: 10 November 2026.

**1. WHY THIS TASK**

Chosen task: Turn a client change request into a revised timeline. Real task: twice a week, about 45 minutes.
Serves: C1 Plans work in a realistic order; C2 Notices what is missing or contradictory and says so; C3 Writes clear, friendly messages to clients; C4 Keeps dates and numbers accurate.
Not shown by this exercise: C5 Keeps several client relationships moving at once. One short task cannot show it; the interviews carry it.
Other tasks considered:
- Build the weekly project schedule from a kickoff note: about 90 minutes, too long for a 40-minute working period; it would serve C1, C2 and C4 but not C3.
- Write the weekly client status email: 30 minutes; serves only C3 and C4.
- Chase overdue client feedback: 20 minutes; serves only C3.
- Reconcile timesheets against project budgets: about 120 minutes, far over the limit, and not in the role description.
- Hand a finished project to invoicing: 60 minutes, longer than the 40-minute working period.

**2. THE EXERCISE**

Scenario: Studio B is making a gift email for Client A, a garden shop. Its schedule has six steps. On Wednesday 9 December the client asks to add a second product range. The candidate revises the timeline and replies to the client.
The candidate is asked to:
1. Write the revised timeline: each step with its start and end date, using what the materials say.
2. List up to five questions or assumptions that the materials leave open.
3. Write a reply to the client in 150 words or fewer.
Materials, as the candidate sees them:
- M1 Current schedule (working days only; each step starts the working day after the previous one ends):
  1. Copy draft: Mon 7 Dec to Wed 9 Dec (3 days)
  2. Client review of copy: Thu 10 Dec to Fri 11 Dec (2 days)
  3. Design: Mon 14 Dec to Wed 16 Dec (3 days)
  4. Client review of design: Thu 17 Dec to Fri 18 Dec (2 days)
  5. Build and test the email: Mon 21 Dec to Tue 22 Dec (2 days)
  6. Send: Wed 23 Dec
- M2 Message from the client, received Wed 9 Dec: "Hello, we would like to add our lantern range to the gift email. The photos arrive on Friday 11 December. It is only a small addition, so we still want it sent on Wednesday 23 December. Our manager, who approves everything, is away from Monday 14 to Wednesday 16 December. We close on Thursday 24 December and reopen on Monday 4 January. Thank you."
- M3 Studio note:
  - Adding a product range adds 1 working day to the copy step and 1 working day to the design step.
  - The steps run one after another, in the order of M1.
  - A client review step needs the client's manager on each of its days. A day when the manager is away does not count.
  - Studio B and this client are both closed from Thu 24 Dec and reopen on Mon 4 Jan.
Still to be supplied: none.
Not needed from the candidate: knowledge of Studio B, its clients or its systems; any tool beyond a word processor or spreadsheet; any lookup outside the materials.
A finished submission is: one document with the three parts, in a file named with the candidate's reference number only.

**3. TIME AND EFFORT**

With the agreed extra time: 90 minutes: 15 reading, 60 working, 15 checking and sending.

Payment: an exercise longer than 60 minutes is paid. This one is 60 minutes, so no payment applies; see open question 2 for the 90-minute version.
Effort check: Studio B will not use the output; the client, dates and steps are invented.

**4. ACCESSIBILITY ARRANGEMENTS**

Needs a decision by the Office Manager: none. Any other request goes to the Office Manager.
The criteria, materials and evidence do not change with an adjustment.

**5. CANDIDATE INSTRUCTIONS**

About this exercise. This is a small task of the kind a Project Coordinator at Studio B does. Every candidate gets the same one, so that we can look at the same kind of work.

What to do. Studio B is making a gift email for a client, Client A. A change has come in. Use the three materials below to (1) write the revised timeline, with each step's start and end date; (2) list up to five questions or assumptions the materials leave open; and (3) write a reply to the client in 150 words or fewer. The client and every detail are invented.

Time. Please spend no more than 60 minutes: about 10 reading, 40 working and 10 checking and sending. If you have arranged extra time, the limit is 90 minutes: 15, 60 and 15. Stop at the limit. We look at what is there, and an unfinished piece is fine.

Materials. M1, M2 and M3 are attached, in large print or plain text on request. You need nothing else.

What to hand in. One document with the three parts, in a file named with the reference number we give you and with your name nowhere in it. Send it by email to recruiting@studio-b.example within 3 working days of receiving this. You may write your answers or record a spoken answer.

What we look at. Whether the timeline puts each step after the one it depends on; whether you notice what is missing or does not fit, and say so; whether your reply to the client is clear and friendly and says what changes and what you need; and whether the dates and figures are right and agree with each other.

What we will not do. We will use your work only for this application. We will not ask you for more unpaid work. We will not judge anything outside the work, such as how fast you send it or how it is laid out.

Adjustments. Is there anything that would help you do this exercise, such as extra time, a different format or a different day? Tell us and we will arrange it.

Questions. Ask the Office Manager at recruiting@studio-b.example.

What happens next. You will hear from us within five working days of sending your work.

**6. EVIDENCE THE PANEL WILL LOOK FOR**

C1 Plans work in a realistic order.
- Clear evidence in the work: a timeline in which each step starts only after the step before it has finished, with a start and end date for every step, and with the extra copy day and the extra design day included.
- Gaps look like: a step missing, steps overlapping with no reason given, or the added days ignored.
- Not evidence: a polished chart in place of dates.

C2 Notices what is missing or contradictory and says so.
- Clear evidence in the work: states that the client's wish to send on Wednesday 23 December does not fit M1 to M3, or says what would have to change for it to fit; notes that the size of the lantern range is not given; notes that the manager's absence affects the review days.
- Gaps look like: accepting "a small addition" without checking it against M3, or answering an open point with a guess presented as fact.
- Not evidence: a long list of questions that all have answers in the materials.

C3 Writes clear, friendly messages to clients.
- Clear evidence in the work: a reply of 150 words or fewer that says what changes, gives a date or the choices, says what is needed from the client, and stays polite.
- Gaps look like: no date or choice given, no request to the client, or a tone that blames the client.
- Not evidence: length, or a particular greeting or sign-off.

C4 Keeps dates and numbers accurate.
- Clear evidence in the work: every date is a working day, the closure from Thursday 24 December to Sunday 3 January is respected, and the dates in the timeline match those in the reply.
- Gaps look like: a step on a weekend or a closed day, or a date in the reply that differs from the timeline.
- Not evidence: the number of decimal places or the style of the date.

Reference points in the materials: one valid answer, with the working, is below. A candidate who states other assumptions can reach a different date; what counts is that the assumptions are stated.
- Copy draft: 4 days (3 plus 1), Mon 7 Dec to Thu 10 Dec.
- Copy review: needs 2 manager days. Fri 11 Dec is day 1; the manager is away Mon 14 to Wed 16 Dec; Thu 17 Dec is day 2.
- Design: 4 days (3 plus 1), Fri 18 Dec, Mon 21, Tue 22 and Wed 23 Dec.
- Closure: Thu 24 Dec to Sun 3 Jan.
- Design review: 2 days, Mon 4 Jan and Tue 5 Jan.
- Build and test: 2 days, Wed 6 Jan and Thu 7 Jan.
- Send: Fri 8 Jan, which is 16 calendar days after the Wednesday 23 December the client asked for.
- The photos arriving on Fri 11 Dec do not hold anything up, because design starts on Fri 18 Dec.
- Not stated in the materials: how many lanterns are in the range.

Not counted for any criterion: how fast the work was returned; layout and formatting beyond what the brief asks; length beyond the stated limits; the variety of English used; anything outside the submission itself.
