# Work sample exercise brief: trial on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (claude-sonnet-5-5). It did not write the first version of the template, but it edited the template on 10 November 2026 after an earlier review, and then ran this trial on the edited version.
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

The inputs are the invented example's own (Studio B, a made-up 30-person studio hiring a Project Coordinator). No client data was used. This record replaces an earlier one made on the first version of the template, because the instruction block, the output format and the review notes changed.

## Inputs used

The six inputs in `public/example.md`: the role description, the five criteria, the task list, the settings, the standing wording and the hiring manager's notes. No list of topics the company never raises was supplied, so the output says nothing about it. The settings give a contact address but no return address.

## What came out

The full output follows. It is a different scenario from the one in the published example (a bakery, a wedding-cake section, April 2027), built from the same inputs.

Status: Draft for approval. Not shared with candidates or the panel.
Prepared for: Head of Delivery. Approves: Head of Delivery. Panel: Head of Delivery; Senior Project Manager. People lead: Office Manager. Decides about each candidate: Head of Delivery. Date: 10 November 2026.

Chosen task: Turn a client change request into a revised timeline. Real task: twice a week, about 45 minutes.
Serves: C1 Plans work in a realistic order; C2 Notices what is missing or contradictory and says so; C3 Writes clear, friendly messages to clients; C4 Keeps dates and numbers accurate.
Not shown by this exercise: C5 Keeps several client relationships moving at once. One task about one client cannot show it; the interviews have to carry it.
Other tasks considered:

- Write the weekly client status email: 30 minutes; serves only C3 and C4.
- Chase overdue client feedback: 20 minutes; serves only C3.
- Reconcile timesheets against project budgets: about 120 minutes, and not in the role description.
- Hand a finished project to invoicing: 60 minutes, longer than the 40-minute working period.

2. THE EXERCISE
Scenario: Studio B is making a spring catering email for Client C, a bakery. The schedule has six steps. On Tuesday 13 April the client asks to add a section on wedding cakes. The candidate revises the timeline and replies to the client.
The candidate is asked to:
  1. Write the revised timeline: each step with its start and end date.
  2. List up to five questions or assumptions that the materials leave open.
  3. Write a reply to the client in 150 words or fewer.
Materials, as the candidate sees them:
  M1 Current schedule (working days only, Monday to Friday; each step starts the working day after the previous one ends):

    2. Client review of copy: Thu 15 Apr to Fri 16 Apr (2 days)
    3. Design: Mon 19 Apr to Tue 20 Apr (2 days)
    4. Client review of design: Wed 21 Apr to Thu 22 Apr (2 days)
    5. Build and test the email: Fri 23 Apr to Mon 26 Apr (2 days)
    6. Send: Tue 27 Apr
  M2 Message from the client, received Tue 13 Apr: "Hello, could we add a section on wedding cakes to the spring catering email? It is only a small addition, so we still want it sent on Tuesday 27 April. Photos of the cakes will come on Friday 16 April. Our owner approves everything on our side and is away on Friday 16 and Monday 19 April. Thank you."
  M3 Studio note:
    - Adding a section adds 1 working day to the copy step and 1 working day to the design step. Other steps do not change.
    - The steps run one after another, in the order of M1.
    - A client review step needs the client's owner on each of its days. A day when the owner is away does not count towards the step; the step pauses and carries on when the owner is back.

Still to be supplied: none.
Not needed from the candidate: knowledge of Studio B or its clients; any tool beyond a word processor or spreadsheet; any lookup outside the materials.
A finished submission is: one document with the three parts, in a file named with the candidate's reference number only. No length limit except the reply (150 words).

With the agreed extra time: 90 minutes: 15 reading, 60 working, 15 checking and sending.
Where, when, how returned: at home, on the candidate's own computer; returned by email within 3 working days of receiving the brief, to the contact address (no return address was given; see Changes).
Payment: an exercise longer than 60 minutes is paid. The exercise is set at 60 minutes, so no payment applies. With the agreed extra time it would run to 90 minutes; the rule does not say whether that counts, so see open question 2. Any payment needs a person's approval.
Effort check: the company will not use the output; the client, cakes and dates are invented.

Asked of every candidate, in the same words, before the exercise is given: "Is there anything that would help you do this exercise, such as extra time, a different format or a different day? Tell us and we will arrange it."
Already agreed: up to 50% more time; materials as large print or plain text; written answers or a recorded spoken answer; a different day inside the window; breaks that do not count against the time.
Needs a decision by Office Manager: none yet. Any other request goes to the Office Manager.

What to do. A client has asked for a change to a schedule. (1) Write the revised timeline, with a start and end date for each step. (2) List up to five questions or assumptions you would raise because the information leaves something open. (3) Write a reply to the client in 150 words or fewer.

6. EVIDENCE THE PANEL WILL LOOK FOR
C1 Plans work in a realistic order.
  Clear evidence in the work: a timeline in which each step starts after the step it depends on has finished, in the order of M1, with dates for every step, including the extra copy day and the extra design day.
  Gaps look like: steps overlapping or reordered; a step missing; no dates; the added days ignored; design starting before the photos.
  Not evidence: a claim to be well organised; a polished chart in place of dates.
C2 Notices what is missing or contradictory and says so.
  Clear evidence in the work: says that "only a small addition" and "still 27 April" clash with M3 and the owner's absence; or names an open point (how many cakes, whether copy for the section exists, the photo format, whether the owner can review on other days) and asks about it or states an assumption.
  Gaps look like: revising the dates silently with no mention that the send date moves; no questions.
  Not evidence: long generic questions not tied to the materials.
C3 Writes clear, friendly messages to clients.
  Clear evidence in the work: a reply of 150 words or fewer that says what changes (the send date), why, and what is needed from the client (photos on 16 April, the owner available on the review days), and stays polite.
  Gaps look like: no new date or choice; no request to the client; blame; a date that differs from the timeline.
  Not evidence: exclamation marks, emoji, or a formal or casual register alone.
C4 Keeps dates and numbers accurate.
  Clear evidence in the work: every date falls on a working day, and the dates in the timeline and in the reply agree.
  Gaps look like: weekend dates; a reply date that differs from the timeline.
  Not evidence: the number of decimal places or the presentation of a table.
Reference points in the materials: one valid answer, with the working. A candidate who states other assumptions can reach a different date; what counts is that the assumptions are stated.

## What was observed

- **Every part was produced.** All nine sections are filled and the template did not stop. The time split is 10, 40 and 10 minutes, and 15, 60 and 15 with the agreed extra time; both add up to the total. The weekdays of every date in the materials and the reference answer were checked afterwards with a calendar program, and the revised send date is four working days after the original.
- **The same task was chosen as in the published example,** the change request, because it serves four of the five criteria. C5 was listed as not shown.
- **The notes were sorted as the template now says.** Every note sits under exactly one of Used, Not used and Declined. The live client project and the request to mark, rank and say who to hire were declined. The two-hour request went under Not used, with a pointer to open question 1. Speed of return was not used and listed under not counted. Nothing was asked to be sent or read. No criterion needed removing, so Removed says "none"; that heading was not exercised by a real case in this run.
- **The new rules were followed where the inputs reached them.** The missing return address was handled by using the contact address and saying so under Changes. The missing place of keeping became a marked gap with a question. The extra-time payment case became an open question to the Office Manager. The instructions point to the materials rather than repeating them. The 60-minute limit gives an exact sixth, so the tie rule for halfway cases was not exercised.
- **Not independent of the example or of the edit.** The model that ran this trial had read the published example's output and the earlier trial record, and had made the edits to the template that it then ran. I wrote a different scenario on purpose, but this run does not show how a fresh reader of the template would fare.
- **Ambiguities and guesses that remain:**
  - Whether the paid rule counts a candidate on agreed extra time is not settled by the inputs. The template now says to raise it; it does not decide it.
  - Whether a paused client review keeps its counted days was not stated in the inputs, so I wrote it into my own materials.
  - "Seen by" names the Office Manager because the Office Manager holds the reference numbers; the settings do not say who else sees submissions beyond the panel. A person should confirm.
- **Facts not in the inputs.** In this output: the bakery, the wedding cakes, every date and the owner's absence, which are the invented material the template tells the assistant to create. The 150-word reply limit and the three-part submission are the assistant's own design for the invented exercise.
- **Shortcomings.** A single try on a single model; I could not check whether a real hiring manager would find the exercise fair. The reviewer checklist was applied only by me. No list of topics the company never raises was supplied, so that input was not exercised.
