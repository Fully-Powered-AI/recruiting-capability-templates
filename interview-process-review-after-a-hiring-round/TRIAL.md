# Interview process review after a hiring round: tried on its example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (model ID claude-sonnet-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

The inputs were the invented example in `public/example.md` (the settings, rubric, interview plan, panel feedback and process issues note). No client data was used. The run followed the instruction block and the output format in `member/template.md`. The run was made without first reading the example's output; the two were compared afterwards. The first run was made once and the output below is as produced. It was followed by changes to the template and a check of the revised version, recorded at the end of this file.

## What was observed

- **Every required part was produced.** Nothing in the run made it stop. All six headers matched, no IDs clashed, no row was unread, and there were three candidates, so no section was cut short.
- **Agreement with the published example.** All counts matched: 24 rows read, 10 notes not usable of 22 rated, four flagged questions with the same order, one repeated question, the six criteria statuses, 2 of 5 spread comparisons with the same largest gaps and codes, two questions not asked, and the same ten notes in section 7. The judging of notes was the same for all 22 asked rows.
- **Where the instructions left room to guess.** (Several lines in this list (here and under "Differences" and "Facts not in the inputs") refer to the example as it stood at the first run: its feedback notes used gendered wording, its section 3 gave the C2 line as "3 of 3; Q3" and its process issue labels were all "not checkable". The example has since been revised, so check those lines against the first run's output above, not against the current `public/example.md`. After the second independent review the tie-break for plan order and the naming of an interview in an added-question revision were also made explicit in the template.)
  - Two notes could be read as either "impression only" or "general statement" ("Said he gets on well with engineers", "Said she works closely with product"). I chose general statement because the candidate said something; the order rule only helps if you read "impression only" as "nothing they said or did". The template does not say what to do with a note that reports what the candidate said but gives no case.
  - K2's Q3 note ("uses last quarter's ticket counts... no example of a shortfall") is borderline. I judged it usable because it names a method and states a limit. A stricter reader could call it a general statement. It does not change any flagged question.
  - The template says to mark an issue as one the files "show", "show something related" or "cannot check", and to give a figure in each case. Issues 2 and 3 are both not checkable and related to a figure. I wrote both, and the example uses only "not checkable". The three labels overlap.
  - The headline says "questions" gave little usable evidence, but step 5 counts pairs of interview and question. The two agree here (four of each) only because Q3 is flagged in one interview.
  - The order for flagged pairs puts ties at "most asked, then plan order". It does not say whether plan order is by interview or by question number; here both give the same result.
  - "Level descriptions" are named in the revisions, but the rubric file has no column for them and no rating scale descriptions are in the inputs. I could draft a revision to brief interviewers on them but could not say what they say.
  - Whether to reword, remove or replace a flagged question is left to the writer. I chose differently from the example in two places (R2 replaces rather than removes the repeated Q3; R5 is separate from the Q6 reword).
  - The template does not say whether a revision that adds a question may name the interview. I left it to the recruiting lead for C6; the example puts it in the Operations panel.
- **Stopping.** I was not told to stop for any reason. The decline rules, the header-mismatch rule and the missing-date rule did not apply to these inputs, so they were not exercised.
- **Differences from the published example.**
  - I used interview IDs (I1, I2, I3) in sections 1 and 2; the example uses interview names. The format asks for "[interview]", so either fits.
  - Section 5: I listed K2 · I2 · Q3 first and numbered each; the example lists K3 · I1 · Q2 first, in one numbered line.
  - Section 6, issues 1 to 3: different wording on what is related or not checkable (see above). The example says Q6 is "the only question for C5"; I did not, though it is true.
  - Section 8: I wrote eight revisions, the example eight, but they differ. Mine replaces rather than removes Q3 in I2, separates the briefing for C5 from the Q6 reword, and has no revision about putting the only question for a criterion first. The example has no separate revision for the C5 briefing and adds R8 (put the only question for a criterion first). My R8 is the rubric session; the example's R7 covers the same.
  - The example's section 3 for C2 says "Q3 in two interviews"; mine gives "Q3". The example's C6 line reads "0 of 3" only.
  - Wording of drafted questions differs; these are drafts either way.
- **Facts not in the inputs.**
  - In my output: none found. Drafted question wording is new text, not a fact. The statement in R2 that C2 is covered 3 of 3 by I1 is counted from the rows.
  - In the example's output: R5 says "in the time freed by R2 and R3". The inputs say nothing about how long the interviews are or how much time is free. The example's R8 and its section 6 note that Q6 is "the only question for C5" are true from the plan.
- **Shortcomings.**
  - The review leans on one judgement: whether a note is usable. The template does list every unusable note for the recruiting lead to check, which is the right safeguard, but the line between "usable" and "general statement" is not sharp, as above.
  - On this tiny round (three candidates, the minimum), 2 of 5 spread comparisons is not a pattern. The template reports it without comment, as it should, and the revision I drafted for it (a session on levels) goes slightly beyond the figure.
  - The run needed every count to be tallied by hand across 24 rows; I rechecked all totals (12 usable, 10 not usable, 2 not asked, 24 read). A model with a different tallying habit could slip, which is why the reviewer checklist begins with the counts.

## After the review: changes to the template and a second check

An independent review of the template found small problems. The first run's output and observations above are left as recorded. The template was then changed:

- The headline now counts interview-question pairs, not questions, because step 5 flags pairs. The first run's headline still reads "4 gave little usable evidence".
- The three labels for a process issue are now defined: the files show it (they record what the issue says), show something related (a figure for the same interview, question or criterion, with what is lacking stated), or cannot be checked (nothing bears on it). In the first run, issues 1 to 3 were all of the second kind; the example's wording has been brought into line.
- A note that reports only what the candidate said, with no case, is now defined as a general statement, which is how the first run judged it.
- Section 4 now ends with a caution: a gap may rest on different questions and evidence, and an interview with one interviewer is identified by its code.
- The rubric may carry an optional `level_descriptions` column; the review never writes level descriptions.

This second check is not an independent run. It was made by the session that revised the template, with the first run and the example in view. It re-read the revised instruction block against the revised output format, and recomputed every count in the review below from the 24 feedback rows: 24 rows read, 22 asked, 2 not asked, 12 usable and 10 not usable, four flagged pairs, 2 of 5 spread comparisons. The review below is the revised example's output; the data is invented and no client data was used.
